## 1. Experience

**Capability, not interface.** Nothing here adds permanent chrome. The tool rail keeps seven buttons, the context bar two to five verbs, a prompt three option chips and `⋯`. Every capability is reached through the slash palette, a handle or the gumball, the inspector, or the assistant.

- **A slash list that stays short.** Opened empty, it shows recent verbs, then the verbs that fit the selection, at most six rows in each of the seven groups. Everything else appears only as it is typed. Rhino names match typed text only.
- **Typing a Rhino name never dead-ends.** `/Zebra`, `/NetworkSrf`, `/SelOpenCrv`: the palette runs the verb if there is one. If the job is done differently here, the row reads like a Notion hint with one button: "*SelOpenCrv* · Here: Select, then type *open curves* · **Do it**". If it is not built, the row says when ("Planned · step 28") or why not ("Left out: drives a measuring arm"), with **Ask the assistant**.
- **Coming from Rhino** (Help): one page, set like a Notion database. Search a Rhino command; see its name here and its state. The top line is the audit, always three numbers: "Rhino 8: 107 done · 12 partly · 5 left out, of 195 · report". Today it would read 16 done; at the end of this plan, 160 done, 30 partly, 5 left out.
- **About → Licences** names the geometry engine, shows its licence and where its source is, and offers "Use a replacement engine file" (4.5).
- **Surfaces** look like construction, not metal: a pale two-sided tint, ink edges, no weight. Open edges are dashed. *Join* or *Thicken* makes metal; when surfaces do not close, the HUD says "3 open edges keep this from becoming metal" with **Show me**.
- **Checks you can see.** View → Check: Thickness, Curvature, Stripes, Draft, Open edges, Clash, Section. One at a time, with a legend chip; Esc leaves. Ramps run ink to `--danger` or between two muted axis hues, never a rainbow.
- **The workshop sheet** is a live page beside CAD: the piece's facts, four dimensioned views, a stone map coloured by size with a legend, the stone list and the checks. Cost lines show on screen and are left off the download unless *Include cost* is on. One button: Download PDF. *For casting* stays off until a caster profile carries a shrink figure; every output then says "Scaled up 1.25 % for shrinkage: do not scale again" (4.11).
- **Weight.** The HUD gains a target: `≈ 3.9 g · target 3.5 g`. *Lighten* offers moves with their saving before anything is cut: "Scoop the band −0.3 g · Open azures under 14 stones −0.2 g".
- **Stone fields by hand.** With a field selected, `p` shows each stone as a point. Drag one and it is pinned; lock, delete, add, mirror. Overlaps are marked. Unpinned stones re-flow and say so: "12 pinned · 61 placed automatically".
- **Someone else's file.** Drop a `.3dm`, STEP or STL: a card says what it found ("A ring, about US 6¼ · mesh · 2 open edges") and offers Repair, Resize, Wrap or Open as reference. A re-size shows what moved and always says "Check the stone seats: a mesh does not tell us where they are."
- **Soft shapes** (SubD) start from Band, Signet, Bezel, Ring, Box or Sphere; a *Smooth* switch shows cage or surface; the mesh badge stays, and *Make NURBS* (if it ships, 4.6) reports how far the result is from the soft shape.
- **Waiting is visible.** A shape that waits on a job (a quad remesh, a fine wrap) shows its input ghosted with a progress line and Cancel; weight is marked "≈", and exports and "Ready to cast" wait, in words.
- **Formulas and drawings.** Type `=` in any number field: `= $width * 0.12`; variables live in one small inspector block. The *History map* (on request) shows the piece as linked cards. A drawing is a sheet on a page: click two points to dimension; views follow the design.

**With the AI.** Each of the above is a verb the assistant calls through `design.run`. It asks first, then acts; the person watches, pauses or takes over as today. "Make this 3.5 g" → it asks the minimum wall, whether the inside stays comfort-fit, and whether azures may open under side stones; then scoops, opens azures, re-checks, and reports grams saved per move, each undoable. It never switches shrink scaling on by itself: that is an approval card.

**General NURBS** (so Rhino geometry can be held, edited and written back).
- Curves: `curve.shape.points` with `style: controlPoints` gains `knots?: number[]` (the full knot vector; absent means clamped uniform, as today), `periodic?`, and keeps `degree` and per-point `w`. A point insert on a curve that has `knots` replaces `knots` in the same patch.
- Surfaces: `surface{net}` = `{net: {type: 'net', degreeU, degreeV, knotsU: number[], knotsV: number[], periodicU, periodicV, rows: Key[], cols: Key[]}, poles: Record<"row.col", {at, w?}>}`. Pole drags are one-key patches. A structural edit (insert or remove a row or column, change degree or knots) replaces `net` whole, because it carries `type` and `guardPatch` restores such an object whole or not at all, and adds or removes pole records in the same change. `validateDesign` requires exactly one pole per row and column pair, so two structural edits that interleave cannot leave a ragged grid: the second is rejected through the existing reject path.

## 4. Hard parts and decisions

### 4.1 Parity is a ledger with three numbers
R22 counted 1,078 Rhino 8 command names in 195 capabilities; 16 are fully in the product today and 142 are not fully covered after step 8 [V R22; recounted by script this session]. Decision: one checked-in ledger maps every name to a verb, a "how it is done here" answer, a planned step, or a leave-out with its reason. A row has a state (`done`, `partly`, `planned`, `leftOut`) and an `accepts` list (curves, surfaces, solids, meshes, soft shapes, annotations, blocks). A row is `done` only when a tagged test passes for every type in `accepts`; until the last type exists it is `partly`. So select-by-type (J3), snaps on objects (I3) and sub-object picks (J5) land in step 15 and turn `done` only as steps 22, 29, 32 and 37 add their object types. Every report gives done, partly and left out together. *Rejected:* a prose claim ("Rhino-class"); one "covered" number, which hid 26 partial rows in this plan's first draft; a command per Rhino command (119 `Sel…` alone). The interface differences in R22 §6 are deliberate, not leave-outs.

### 4.2 Surfaces as objects (this changes `freeform-cad.md` §4 item 7)
Rhino treats an open surface as an ordinary object; about two dozen rows wait on that [E R22].

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Keep "bodies are closed"; a `surface` node with built-in thickness | Rejected: cannot express Untrim, ExtractSrf, Join, MatchSrf, BlendSrf, which act on open faces |
| Bodies may be open (a flag) | Rejected: weight, checks, seats and casting exports would each have to learn "maybe open"; `checkGeometry` rejects open shells today |
| **Surfaces are their own class, with `SHAPE_KINDS` for the fields that only move, copy or cut** | Recommended |

**Decision.** `surface` and `surfaceOp` make and edit surfaces; `solidify` is the only bridge to metal (sew with `BRepBuilderAPI_Sewing`, then a solid; cap; or thicken) and fails per node with the free-edge list the sewing class returns [V type file, this session]. `surface{fromFaces}` is the bridge back. Weight, "Ready to cast" and STL see bodies only; STEP, IGES and .3dm carry surfaces as surfaces. Array, mirror, flow, deform and a boolean split accept surfaces through `SHAPE_KINDS` (§3), so the ledger cannot show Mirror or Flow "done" while they refuse a surface. Stones may be laid out on a surface; seats and checks wait for metal. A soft shape with an open cage is a surface. The rule becomes **"metal is closed"**. The class does have consumers to change, and §3 "Kernel types" lists them.

### 4.3 General NURBS
The first draft's surface grid had no knot vectors and no order, so an imported Rhino surface could not be held, knot commands had nothing to act on, and the round-trip test could not pass. Decision: the `net` and `knots` fields of §3. Per-pole edits stay independent; structure is replaced whole. *Rejected:* keyed rows and columns with ranks, which a row insert by one author and a column insert by another leave ragged (the bug class `freeform-cad.md` §4 item 2 found with `order`); a flat pole array, which makes two people's pole drags collide. Proof: a guarded-undo property test like the curve one (§5).

### 4.5 The LGPL duties of shipping the engine (owed today, not only for our own build)
This is not legal advice; it sets out what the texts say and what counsel must confirm. Sources read this session: LGPL 2.1 sections 2, 4, 5 and 6 from the licence copy shipped inside the engine package (gnu.org would not load), and the Open CASCADE exception, version 1.0, from the OpenCascade repository [V].

**What the texts require.**
- The `.wasm` is the Library in object form. **Section 4:** whoever distributes it accompanies it with the complete corresponding machine-readable source, or, when the binary is offered from a designated place, offers equal access to the source from the same place.
- **Section 2,** for a modified or rebuilt Library: it stays a library; changed files carry prominent notices of the change and its date; the whole is licensed to everyone at no charge under the LGPL. Our build list, shims and patches are part of that whole.
- **Section 6,** for the product that works with it: the product may ship under our own terms only if those terms "permit modification of the work for the customer's own use and reverse engineering for debugging such modifications". Each copy needs a prominent notice that the Library is used and is under the LGPL, a copy of the licence, and the Library's copyright notice among any the app displays. And one of: the Library's source together with what a user needs to run the product with a modified Library; a shared-library mechanism that uses a copy already on the user's computer and works with a modified, interface-compatible one; a written offer valid three years; or equal access from the same place.
- **The exception** covers only material from OpenCascade's header files compiled into the object code of a work that uses the Library, on condition of a prominent notice in the documentation. It lifts none of the duties above.

**Where the product stands** [V]. The stock engine ships today. `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` says it ships as a separate, replaceable `.wasm`, and the kernel does load it by URL or bytes (`configureRuntime`). But the root `LICENSE` grants no right to modify anything, no screen or file gives the notice, the engine package carries no exception file, and no source is offered. A browser that loads the studio from a hosted server receives the `.wasm`: treat that as distribution [E].

**What "replace the library" means for a WebAssembly bundle** [E, for counsel to confirm]. The engine is one file with a published interface (its typings). A user can replace it if the product documents and supports loading another file in its place: `PAVE_OCCT_WASM` for an install and its server, and "Use a replacement engine file" in About → Licences for the studio in a browser.

**Deliverables, each in a named step with a check.**

### 4.7 Jobs, QuadRemesh, ShrinkWrap
- **A node that waits on a job has no shape yet.** It shows its input ghosted, for display only; it yields no body, so weight is marked "≈" and `job.pending` blocks the production check, exports and "Ready to cast". When the job ends, one op writes `result: {assetId, inputHash}` into the node; the result is an asset of the piece and travels in the `.pave` file. Evaluation uses it while `inputHash` matches; otherwise `job.stale` blocks until it is re-run. Mesh-to-cage writes a new `subd` node as ops. Nothing is recomputed on another machine, so a native build here and WebAssembly there cannot disagree. *Rejected:* "evaluates to its input until done" (the first draft: a wrong shape for minutes, and for ever with jobs off); a cache keyed by hash outside the piece (no tenant boundary, and a recompute elsewhere).
- **QuadRemesh:** QuadriFlow's watertight guarantee needs a SAT step that shells out to `minisat` and `timeout` and is Unix only [V]. Decision: a native sidecar on the server is the main route (spike P6); a WebAssembly build without the SAT step is the second route. The job checks its own output (closed, manifold, all quads) and fails otherwise; today's `repairMesh` is not used, because it takes triangles and may rebuild by level set [V code]. No symmetry or guide curves at first, so row E6 ends `partly`. *Rejected:* QuadWild (GPL-3); Blender's remesher (ties CAD to Blender being installed); Instant Meshes (quad-dominant [E]).
- **ShrinkWrap:** the engine exists: `mesh/resurface.ts` rebuilds a surface as a level set, 150 cells across, 0.05 mm floor, on a dense grid of a float and a byte per cell [V code]. Decision: `meshOp{shrinkWrap}` with offset, target edge (the grid step), smoothing and hole filling. The browser keeps 150 cells. A finer local wrap is a job with a budget of 20 million cells (about 100 MB [E arithmetic]); over it, the edge is coarsened and the person is told the edge used. Wraps at 0.05 mm on large pieces need a sparse grid (OpenVDB, Apache 2.0, server only [V]) and wait for hosted workers. *Rejected:* CGAL Alpha Wrapping (GPL or a paid licence [V]).

### 4.11 Jewelry decisions and casting safety
- **Scoop** is a second sweep with each profile inset by the wall, limited to an arc and faded at its ends (profile inset is the robust way to hollow [V `freeform-cad.md` §4 item 4]). **Azures** are one lofted cutter per stone, cut at production like seats (Manifold took 2,000 cutters in 1.6 s [V R12]), only where metal behind the stone ends within `depthMax`; the opening at the stone end never exceeds the drill hole.
- **Pins** are keyed records the layout solver treats as fixed, so a designer's pin and the assistant's undo separately; unpinned stones have no identity, and the screen says so. Pins on today's template fields need nothing from steps 1 to 8 (step 13).
- **A set is two linked pieces.** The band is sized, weighed, priced and exported alone. It stores its own contour, so it never changes because someone edited the ring: it offers **Update**.
- **Shrink scaling can spoil a casting.** Today the RFQ tells the caster to apply their own shrinkage [V `rfq.ts`], the default profile says the caster compensates [V], and R13's evidence is that bureaus scale files themselves; a scaled file sent with that sentence is scaled twice, and an STL carries no mark. Rules: the figure comes only from the caster profile's `shrinkLinearPct`, which has no default (the "typical" figures in `metals.ts` are never used to scale); the scale is 1 / (1 − s); it warns above 3 % and is refused above 8 % [E]; the first use for a profile needs the percentage typed; one function writes the statement into the RFQ (replacing "apply your own"), the manifest, the sheet's title block and the file name (`…_shrink-1.25pct.stl`); a profile whose caster compensates refuses it. It stays off until the founder says who casts (decision 10).
- **A sprue is a suggestion a caster reviews,** and the sheet says so. It defaults to the thickest section; `sprue.thin` and `sprue.onDetail` warn. Casting trees stay out.
- **Re-sizing a foreign ring** is a mesh warp of the lower arc only; the honest limit is about two sizes [E R13], and no vendor files were available to test. A mesh carries no stone data, so the step looks for holes, seats and relief in the warped arc by curvature and wall checks, warns `resize.detail`, shows a deviation map, re-runs the wall check, and always shows `resize.seatsUnknown` when the arc has holes. It never claims the seats still fit.

### 4.12 Rhino files both ways
rhino3dm reads a B-rep's faces, underlying surfaces, loops and edges, but a trim gives only its edge index, direction and vertices: there are no 2D trim curves [V type file]. So each trimmed face is rebuilt in OpenCascade from its surface and 3D edge loops, with the 2D curves re-projected by `ShapeFix_Face`; seams of closed surfaces and poles are the weak spots [E], which is why spike P3 covers a closed band, a sphere cap and a face with holes. It cannot join faces or build a general trimmed face [V R12; E], so exporting joined solids needs our own openNURBS build with a small shim (spike P4; openNURBS's licence text and its bundled FreeType are read there, since only "no copyleft" on the vendor's page is verified [V R22]). **Order:** step 19 imports meshes, curves, layers and the face meshes a file carries (a file saved small carries none: `import.noMesh`); step 23 imports exact geometry; step 24 exports it, conditional on P4. Until then STEP is the exact route. **Rule:** a later step that adds an object type adds it to .3dm reading and writing in the same step, with one stated exception: soft shapes as real SubD objects wait for step 35.
