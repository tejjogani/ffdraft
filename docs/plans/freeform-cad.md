# Freeform CAD: modeling from scratch with Rhino's toolset, in the studio's calm look

Build brief: the judged synthesis of two independent design proposals ("direct modeling first, history underneath" and "history first"), revised after a round of kernel spikes and a design review (2026-11-24). Tool inventory and feasibility: [`docs/research/12-rhino-parity.md`](../research/12-rhino-parity.md). It builds on the CAD workspace ([`cad-workspace.md`](./cad-workspace.md)) and the assistant ([`assistant.md`](./assistant.md)). Labels: `[V]` checked in the repo or by a spike this session; `[S]` a web fact not opened at source; `[E]` judgement. What the spikes changed is listed in §4, "Repo and spike facts that changed this plan".

## 0. Does it offer all the Rhino features?

**No, and it should not try to.** It offers every Rhino tool a jewelry designer uses weekly or more, with Rhino's command names typed into the command bar landing on Pavé's tool, and it leaves out general-industry surfacing that jewelry rarely needs.

- **What parity means here.** A Rhino jeweler can build any ring, pendant, earring or bracelet from a blank piece with curves, snaps, construction planes, typed numbers, the gumball, extrude, revolve, loft, sweep (with silhouette laws for cathedrals), pipe, booleans, edge fillets, shell, arrays, mirror and flow. Rhino's names (`ExtrudeCrv`, `Sweep2`, `FilletEdge`, `ArrayPolar`, `PointsOn`, `Flow`) are aliases of Pavé's verbs. Everything a person does, the AI can do through the same verbs (`design.run`) and ops.
- **What it does not do by hand.** Organic blended shoulders come from profile interpolation and silhouette laws on one sweep, not from NetworkSrf, BlendSrf or SubD. **Carved and sculpted tops** (a crest, a lion's head) go through `relief` draped on a host face (step 6, a mesh with the badge) or the image-to-3D route, not through hand modeling. Rounding where a separate head meets a band is built into the head or the profile (Layer 0, §4.3); an exact fillet on a fused seam is offered but best effort, because the spikes found it mostly fails [V].
- **The count** (research 12, §3, frequency A and B rows of core Rhino): 74 tools. 22 Pavé covers today **on template pieces** (`ringShank` and friends), mostly as jewelry nodes that replace the plug-ins (ring builder, gems, heads, halo, rows, pavé, cutters, engraving, findings, weight, casting checks). **On drawn bodies**, seated heads, rows, halos and inside-band engraving work from step 3 (a generic stone host and ring data published by ring-rail sweeps, §4.10); before that they work only on templates. Steps 1–8 add 42. 8 are deferred. 2 are not planned. Of 21 plug-in capabilities, 12 are replaced today (on templates) and 5 land in steps 3–7.
- **Which tools come when:**

| 2 Drawing | CPlanes incl. plane on a curve, grid, Osnaps (End, Mid, Cen, Quad, Int, Perp, Tan, OnCrv, plus stone and head points), Ortho, typed coordinates, PointsOn/F10, InsertPoint/InsertKink, tangent arcs inside a curve, live Symmetry, CloseCrv, Offset, FilletCorners, Enter/Space/right-click finish and repeat, a two-up view |

- **Deferred (Later):** NetworkSrf, EdgeSrf and Patch (as one closed `surface` node), Project and Pull, FlowAlongSrf, four-up viewports and named views, curvature and zebra display, blocks, MoveFace/push-pull of arbitrary faces, Make2D, 3DM import, SubD as a mesh (`smoothOut` + `refine`, badged).

## 1. Experience

**Principles.** One way to model: a drag, a typed command, a template and an AI tool call all end in `design.apply` ops (verbs compile to them, for people in the studio and for the AI through `design.run`), so every gesture is undoable, attributed and replayable as JSON. Modeless: no sketch mode or part mode. Select-first and verb-first both work. One gesture, one labelled history line ("Extruded Head 2.40 mm", "Moved point d of Profile"). Numbers are always typeable. Calm by subtraction: nothing floats unless it concerns the current selection or running command.

**Empty piece.** A fifth door, first in reading order for designers: **Start from a sketch**, "Draw curves and build the piece from them." An inline segmented control asks *What are you making?* Ring · Pendant · Earring · Other, which writes `setKind` and, for Ring, one `curve{shape: ring, size US 6}` on the Front plane (with today's ring-size chip, `viewport/Dimensions.tsx`); Pendant/Earring seed nothing but a framed Front view; Other seeds nothing. The camera goes square-on, the grid fades in, the tool rail appears, and the command bar shows *Type a command, or press /*. The four existing doors stay; drawn bodies can be added to any template too.

**A signet ring from scratch** (about 9 nodes; the AI's run in §1 "The AI" writes the same). The spikes showed that a separate head unioned onto the band and rounded with an exact r 0.6 fillet is not dependable (§4.3), so the worked signet makes its shoulders and head from one sweep and its face from a planar cut; nothing in it needs edge names.
1. Ring rail: `curve{ring, US 6}`; its seam is at 6 o'clock, so `at 0` is the bottom and `at 0.5` the top.
2. Band profile on the Right plane: six keyed points (three drawn, three mirrored by `symmetry`), the inner points smooth for a comfort fit's **rounded inside**, the outer corners sharp; its inner midpoint (the anchor) sits on the rail.
3. Shoulder and head profiles: duplicate, widen and raise (head 10 × 4.2 mm).
4. `sweep{rails:[ring], profiles:[{prof, at:0}, {shoulder, at:0.35}, {head, at:0.5}], symmetric: true}`, closed, no caps; `symmetric` mirrors the 0.35 station to 0.65, so each shoulder is placed once.
5. Face plane: `4` (Top), *Plane on curve*, pick the rail at its top, offset `3.4`: `plane: attached{ring, station{at 0.5}, offset 3.4}`, 3.4 mm above the finger at every size.
6. `/rect` 20 × 20 on that plane, `/extrude` up 5 with *Cut*: `extrude` + `boolean{difference, target: sweep, tools:[extrude]}`; the signet face is the planar section of the head.
7. `/stone`, click the face: `gem{placement: surface}` with the cut body as its host (the generic stone host, step 3), flush (gypsy) by default, the usual signet setting; a bezel instead roots its seat into the host the way ring heads do.
8. HUD weight, carats, "Ready to cast" after the production check.
9. Resize to US 7: the rail, the attached profiles and face plane, the sweep, the cut and the stone re-evaluate; the test checks the inner diameter is the size, the face is still 3.4 mm above the inner surface, and no metal lies inside the bore.
Optional, a square-faced signet: `/rect` 8 × 8, `r 1.2` on the same attached plane, `/extrude` down into the band with `taper 6, roundEnd 0.3, flare 0.6` (the head-to-band round built into the head, Layer 0), `/union`. An exact `fillet` on the union's seam (`edges: ["join:<unionId>"]`) is still offered; it runs in the exact worker, best effort, and a failure leaves the flared head as it is.
Optional: draw a heart on the shoulder, `/extrude` with *Cut through*, `/mirror` (`pattern{mirror}` about the YZ plane).

**Finding tools.** One verb registry feeds five entries:
- **Command bar** (bottom centre; while a command runs it **replaces** the next-step chips row, and the HUD collapses to grams). `/` opens it; `/` on the canvas opens the same list as a menu at the cursor, grouped like Notion's block menu (Draw · Shape · Solid · Edit · Arrange · Jewelry · View). Each row: Pavé title, a one-line description in `fg-subtle`, the Rhino alias right-aligned in Geist Mono. After a verb is chosen the bar becomes Rhino's prompt line: the prompt, at most three option keywords (the rest under `⋯`), the typed buffer on the right. An option toggles when its underlined letter is typed **alone** and Enter pressed, as in Rhino, so `d` Enter toggles an option while `d2.5` Enter is a diameter. Enter, Space or a right-click without drag finishes the command; straight after one, Space or Enter on an empty bar repeats it, and a right-click opens the canvas menu with *Repeat <verb>* as its first row, above the studio's *Ask AI…*.
- **⌘K** stays global and includes every verb (aliases in `Command.keywords`, already matched by `command-palette.tsx:90` [V]).
- **Tool rail**: a vertical glass cluster on the viewport's left edge, seven buttons: Select, Curve, Shape (circle, rect, polygon, ring), Solid (extrude, revolve, loft, sweep, wire), Edit (round edges, shell, flow, deform), Arrange (array, mirror, combine), Measure. A click runs the group's last-used tool; the small corner caret (or a long press) opens the group popover, which can be pinned as a floating palette. *Rejected:* a flat top-centre rail of nine tools, which grows with every verb and crowds the existing top toolbar.
- **Context bar**: a glass pill **in the command bar row**, right of the bar, by default, with the two to five verbs that fit, one live number, and "✦ Ask". A preference, *Near selection*, places it 12 px above the selection's screen box instead; there it hides during camera moves and returns at the frozen offset. Closed curve: Extrude · Revolve · Pipe · Offset. Several curves: Loft · Sweep. Body: Round edges · Shell · Combine · Array. Edges: Round · Chamfer. During a command it carries the command's live fields (compact `FieldControl`).
- **Inspector** (generated, flat): new widgets `points` (a table of keyed points, 28 px mono rows, hover highlights the point, corner/smooth/arc toggle), `edges` (removable pills plus "Pick in view"; a pill that matched nothing is struck through with a warning dot), `plane`, `curveRef`, `law` (a small 1D graph of keyed values over `at`).

Numeric grammar (`model/parse.ts`): `2.5` a value; `2.5,1` plane coordinates; `@2,0` or `r2,0` relative; `<45` angle lock; `d2.5` diameter; arithmetic (`17.3/2`). mm, typed to 3 decimals, shown to 2. A lone letter plus Enter is an option toggle; a letter followed by digits is grammar (parse tests cover `d`, `r`, `c`, `<` and `@`). *Rejected:* `E` for Extrude (it is the plan's rotate key), `F` meaning fillet when edges are selected (one key with two meanings), Space opening the command bar (in Rhino it finishes and repeats), and first-letter toggles on every keystroke (they collide with `d2.5` and `r2,0`).

**Construction planes.** The drawing plane follows the view preset, as each Rhino viewport has its own CPlane: `2` Front, `3` Right, `4` Top; the hero view keeps the last one. While a drawing tool runs, the context bar shows *Plane* (Top · Front · Right · On curve · Custom). **Attached planes come first, not last:** a curve drawn over a rail or a body defaults to `plane: attached{node, station|face|stone, offset}`, so it rides on its host when the host changes; *Plane on curve* (step 2) gives the frame perpendicular to a rail at a station, for closed lofts and profiles around the finger. A snap is not a constraint: a curve drawn on a world plane and snapped to the Quad stays where the Quad was. "Plane to face" (step 5) and stone planes (girdle, table, step 3) are attached planes too. The two-up view (`\`) shows the drawing plane square-on beside the perspective, so shoulder points can be edited in both.

**The look** (house rules, `packages/ui/src/styles.css:1-11`: jewelry is the colour, hairlines over shadows, glass only on floating chrome, one blue for selection, one gold for AI, Inter 13 px on a 4 px grid, Geist Mono numbers).
- Curves 1.25 px `--fg` at 70 %, 2 px on hover, `--select` when selected; curves consumed by a solid in `--fg-subtle`, hidden with the *Show construction curves* toggle (step 2). Control polygon 1 px dashed `--fg-subtle`. Points 6 px squares, white with a 1 px ink border, filled `--select` when selected; mirrored (symmetry) points hollow. Rubber band dashed 4/3.
- Faces: picked 14 % `--select` tint with boundary edges in `--select`; hover 8 %. Pickable edges appear only within 8 px of the pointer on the selected body, so a dense ring never turns into a wireframe. No glow, no outline pass.
- Grid drawn in the shader on the active plane: `--line` dots at 1 mm, hairlines at 5 mm, switching to 0.1/0.5 mm when the view is under 6 mm across, fading out over 40 mm. Axes and gumball arrows use three new muted tokens, `--axis-x` rose `#c9877f`, `--axis-y` sage `#86a88a`, `--axis-z` slate `#8f9bb8` (well apart from `--select`); nothing else gets a hue.
- Snaps: only the winning one shows, as a hollow ink glyph (square End, triangle Mid, circle Cen, diamond Quad, × Int) plus a 20 px badge in Geist Mono 11 px ("Quad"). The cursor readout `x 0.00 z −8.26 · 1.10 mm` is a tag; Tab turns it into fields.
- **Glass** (`glass` utility, blur 24 px, saturate 170 %, opaque fallbacks) only on the large chrome: tool rail, command bar (with its prompt line), slash menu, context bar and pinned palettes, so at most four blurred surfaces are on screen at once. Small things over a moving, specular scene are muddy as glass [E], so the snap badge, cursor and gumball readouts, handle tags and the HUD are opaque (`--glass-strong` with no blur, or solid `--bg`). Outliner and inspector stay flat and opaque. Radii 8 for bars, 12 for the slash menu, 6 for chips. `styles.css` line 7–8 allows glass on "the sidebar": that is the app's docked navigation sidebar (`glass-pane`), not the CAD outliner or inspector, which are content panels; step 2 adds one comment line there saying so.
- Outliner: by default the **final bodies** (Rhino's object list): one row per body that is not consumed, with its metal and a mesh badge if it fell back. A *History* toggle shows the chain, modifiers nested over what they modify (`Round edges 0.6 › Combine › Sweep · Extrude`) and curves under the solids that use them in `fg-subtle` with a dotted curve icon, like a Notion toggle list.

**Screens** (the screenshot matrix, §5):
- *A, blank ring sketch:* outliner with one row "Ring size · US 6"; Front view, grid, the ring circle ø 16.51 mm with its chip; left tool rail; command bar placeholder; HUD hidden until there is metal; inspector shows the curve (ring size, plane, offset) or, with nothing selected, a **Drawing** section: plane, grid step, snap chips, ortho switch (workspace preferences, not design data).

While the exact worker is still trying, the field shows a 1 px progress hairline and "Rounding…". Inspector: "Round edges · 3 edges", each edge in plain words ("top edge of the outer face").
- Plus the slash menu, point edit with the gumball on a point selection, and the two-up view.

**Edit in place and handles.** Double-click a body to light up the curves it is made from on their planes, points visible, everything else dimmed to 40 %; Esc leaves. Fields with a `handle` meta draw a handle on the model: extrude's end cap cone edits `distance`, a disc on a fillet edits `radius`, shell's wall arrow edits `thickness`. Dragging previews `update` on that one field and commits one change; the value shows in an opaque tag, ticking at whole and half mm.

**The AI does the same.** Its tools are the command registry. It runs verbs with `design.run` (the same `VerbDef.build` the studio uses, on the server), writes nodes directly with `design.apply` where no verb fits, checks with `design.evaluate`, looks with `viewport.capture`. New: `design.run`, `design.inspect` (named faces and edges with plain words, positions, areas, normals; curve points; measures; what a selector would match) and `viewport.pick` (a pixel of a capture view → node and sub-shape), and sub-shape refs in `ui.context` and `viewport.select`, so "round these edges" means the designer's selection. Prompt rule: use `ringShank`, `setting`, `halo` and `gemRow` first, and drop to curves only for what they cannot express (a custom profile, an organic outline); "Make editable" (step 6) is the bridge. "Make me a tapered comfort band, size 7, with a 2 mm rounded square signet" is: `design.run` (ring rail, profile with symmetry, shoulder and head profiles, sweep) → `design.run` (rect on an attached plane, extrude with flare, union) → `design.evaluate` (applies any `fix.ops`) → `viewport.capture`. The designer watches as today: gold sweep on new nodes, the command bar showing "✦ Assistant · Sweep — Profile along Ring size", the chip with **Undo run** (`design.revert {runId}`). Right-click a face → "Ask AI…" sends its sub-shape ref (while a command runs, a right-click finishes it instead). The verb registry's one-line descriptions and Rhino names are exported for the assistant's prompt.

**Failure, in plain words.** A modifier that cannot be built passes its input through and reports why, with a fix when there is one, so weight, stones and exports still work and "Ready to cast" waits. A body that became a mesh says so where it matters ("This Combine became a mesh, so its edges cannot be rounded. Round the parts before combining them."). A slow exact step shows "Rounding…" or "Combining…" with a hairline, never a frozen view. A selection that no longer exists announces it once in the live region. A node that crashes the engine twice in a row is marked failed ("This operation crashed the geometry engine") and is not retried on every keystroke.

## 2. Structure

**`packages/schema/src/design/`**
- `nodes.ts`: the kinds in §3, `CURVE_KINDS`, `BODY_KINDS` extended (incl. `pattern`), `nodeRefs` extended; `gemRow.host`, `placement.ringTop.shank` and engraving hosts accept any node that publishes ring data (§4.10).
- `verbs.ts` (new, pure): `VerbDef {id, title, group, aliases (Rhino names), describe, writes: NodeKind, prompts: Prompt[], options, build(picks, options, design) → Op[]}`; `Prompt {key, pick: 'curve'|'body'|'edges'|'faces'|'point'|'number', min, max, text}`. Read by the command bar, context bar, tool rail, ⌘K, the assistant's prompt and `design.run`.
- `topo.ts` (new): `SubRef` grammar (`nodeId`, `nodeId/pt:<key>`, `nodeId/pt:<key>~m` for a mirrored point, `nodeId/edge:<name>`, `nodeId/face:<name>`), face and edge name grammar incl. `join:<booleanId>` edge tags, `matchSelector`, `describeSub` (plain words for history, inspector and the AI).
- `clone.ts` (new): `cloneNodes(design, ids) → Op[]` with fresh ids and remapped refs; `rewriteRefs(design, from, to) → Op[]` for "Make editable".
- `rank.ts` (new): fractional ranks between two neighbours, for keyed points and law points.
- `fields.ts`: widgets and the `handle` meta. `describe.ts`: labels for point patches and sub-shape lists.

**`packages/kernel/src/`**
- `geometry/curve.ts` (pure TS, no WASM; exported as `@pave/kernel/curve` so the studio's main thread draws live curves): every curve shape to cubic Bézier spans (rational quadratic for `arc` points) with segment keys; symmetry mirrored at evaluation; control-point style over `geometry/bspline.ts`; smooth style as Catmull-Rom tangents into `blendSegment` (`geometry/loop.ts`); display polylines; analytic snap points; planar offset; `planeFrame`, incl. rail station frames. Spike S9 measured it at 4.2 kB gzipped and 0.23 ms p95 per frame for 400 points [V].
- `features/{curve, curveOp, extrude, loft, sweepFeature, fillet, shell, flow, deform, brepBody}.ts`, registered in `features/index.ts`.
- `topology/names.ts` (provenance names; carried through `BRepAlgoAPI_*` by `Modified`/`Generated`/`IsDeleted`; a boolean tags its `SectionEdges()` as `join:<booleanId>`; ordinals for split faces; hash maps keyed by shape hash), `topology/select.ts` (`resolve(named, selector) → {edges, missing, ambiguous, rebound}`), `topology/facts.ts` (per-face and per-edge facts), `topology/regularity.ts` (crease edges from `BRepLib.EncodeRegularity` and `BRep_Tool.Continuity`).
- `solid/occt.ts`: `prismNamed`, `filletGuarded` (guard and wall-time ladder, §4.4), `hollowByProfile` (revolve and sweep family) and `thickSolidNamed` (prisms), inside the existing `scoped()` and `occtError()`.
- `jewelry/host.ts` (new): a generic `StoneHost` for any body (ray cast on its mesh, the way `bail.ts` finds metal), and `ShankData` published by sweeps whose rail is a ring curve.
- `inspect.ts`: `Kernel.inspect(design, nodeId, what)`, served by the studio worker and the server kernel.
- `export/rhino3dm.ts`: NURBS curves, untrimmed NURBS faces (periodic for closed bands), trimmed planar caps.

**Sweep semantics** (step 3; these were unsaid and broke resizing):
- Profiles are read in the **rail's local frame**: origin on the rail at the profile's station, `u` outward (away from the ring's centre on a ring rail), `v` along the ring's axis. Each profile's `anchor` point (default: the midpoint between its two innermost points) lies on the rail. A profile's own plane is irrelevant to the sweep, and when a profile is added to a sweep its plane becomes `attached{rail, station{at}}` in the same change, so what you see is what is swept and it moves with the rail.
- `at` is stored, typed or dragged in the inspector; when a profile is added it is initialised from where its anchor touches the rail (closest rail parameter). The ring curve's seam is at 6 o'clock (`at 0` bottom, `0.5` top).
- `laws` scale and shift the interpolated profile along `at`: a `Law` is either a drawn curve (`height`: a Front-view outer outline read radially from the rail; `width`: a Top-view outline read across the band) or keyed values `{points: Record<Key, {at, value, rank}>}`. A ring rail plus a Front outline as the height law, an optional Top outline as the width law and `inner: pinned` (inner surface kept on the size circle) is the **silhouette sweep** jewelers draw cathedrals with. `symmetric` mirrors stations and laws about `at 0.5`.
- Two rails (step 7): rail 2 is matched to each station by the closest point in that station's section plane, with the seams aligned first, never by parameter (parameter matching put the surface 2.71 mm off rail 2 and lost two thirds of the volume in spike S1 [V]); OCCT's `MakePipeShell` auxiliary mode is not used (it throws, S1 [V]).
- Profiles with different point counts are matched **by key**: the segments of the profile with fewer points are split (de Casteljau) at the keys the other profile inserted, and a split segment's halves take the inserting profile's keys, so every face keeps one segment key (S6: valid, 10 faces, 102 ms [V]). `ThruSections` is not a naming fallback: it pairs edges by arc length, many-to-many (S6 [V]); it is used only for meshes and unnamed output, and selectors on such output are blocked with `names.none`.

**Extended:** `revolve` gains `curve?` and `axis` (exactly one of `profile`/`curve`); `pipe` gains `path?` (exactly one of `points`/`path`); `pattern` gains `type: curve{path, count, align}` and `grid{countX, countY, spacingX, spacingY}`, `axis` gains `frame{origin, direction}`, mirror gains a plane; `boolean.op` gains `split` (step 6), and a `pattern` among a boolean's tools contributes its copies as separate disjoint tools (the existing one-compound cut path); `relief` gains `outline: curveRef` and `host?: {node, face}` (step 6, draped by a Manifold warp, mesh badge); `group` gains `color?`, `locked?` (left out of the hash like `name`, `evaluate/hash.ts`). `BODY_KINDS` gains `extrude, loft, sweep, fillet, shell, flow, deform, brepBody, pattern` (pattern was missing: `validateDesign` rejected a polar array of beads as a cutter, ff-kernel [V]). Being a body kind makes a node a valid ref; seated stones, rows and inside engraving also need the host data of §4.10.

**Shared types** (`nodes.ts`, `topo.ts`):
- `PointKey = /^[a-z][a-z0-9]{0,7}$/` (never `__proto__`); at most 400 points per curve. **No `order` array:** a point's position in the curve is its `rank` (a fractional-index string; ties broken by key), so inserting or deleting a point touches one key only. The start of a closed curve is its lowest rank.
- `Plane = world{name: top|front|right, offset} | frame{origin, normal, xAxis} | attached{node, station{at} | face{name} | stone{girdle|table|culet}, offset} | none` (3D curve). `station` frames come from any curve (Plane on curve).
- `EdgeSelector`/`FaceSelector = string[1..200]`: names, `join:<booleanId>` tags or patterns with one `*` per part, plus `!`-prefixed exceptions.
- `validateDesign`: curve fields point at `CURVE_KINDS`, body fields at `BODY_KINDS`; every point record has a `rank`; `symmetry` and `onSize` keys exist; `plane.attached.node` is a ref (so a curve on a girdle evaluates after the stone) and cannot depend on its own dependents; `shell` has `open` or `closed`.
- **No schema version bump:** new fields are optional with "exactly one of" rules; old documents parse unchanged.
- `fields.ts`: widgets `points`, `edges`, `faces`, `plane`, `curveRef`, `law`; meta `handle?: {kind: distance|radius|thickness, face?: string, along?: normal|axis}`.

**Ops: none new.** Keyed points with per-key ranks make a point drag, an insert and a delete each an ordinary merge patch on one key (`{shape: {points: {d: {at}}}}`, `{points: {e: {at, rank}}}`, `{points: {e: null}}`), whose inverse `diffPatch` already computes and whose undo `guardPatch` already restricts to what is unchanged (`core.ts:255-276` [V]). With the first design's shared `order` array, undoing an insert after a concurrent insert left `order` and `points` disagreeing, which `validateDesign` rejects (ff-undo-order, run on the real `guardedInverse` and `mergePatch` [V]). "Convert to points" on a `circle`, `rect`, `polygon` or `ring` is one `update` that replaces `shape` whole (it carries `type`, so `guardPatch` restores it whole or not at all); on a ring an option, *Keep on size*, fills `onSize` with the inner points, which then stay projected onto the size circle as the size changes.

**Commands** (`api/commands.ts`, `api/client-tools.ts`):
- `design.run` (mutation, `tool: true`, new): `{pieceId, verb, picks: (SubRef | vec3 | number)[], options, changeId}` → `{ops, nodeIds, diagnostics}`. Runs `VerbDef.build` on the server and applies the result as one change, so the AI gets Duplicate's id remapping, Make editable, Mirror about a plane, Orient3Pt, Align, baked non-uniform scale and station inference without re-implementing them.
- `design.inspect` (query, `tool: true`): `{pieceId, nodeId, what: 'topology'|'curve'|'measure'|'resolve', selector?, between?: [SubRef, SubRef]}` → `{faces: {name, words, kind, area, centroid, normal}[], edges: {name, words, length, mid, convex, crease}[], points?, matches?: {names, missing, ambiguous}, measure?}`. Its description tells the model to call it before writing a selector. Served by the server kernel, so it works with the studio closed.
- `viewport.pick` (client tool, new): `{pieceId, view: CameraPreset|{azimuth, elevation}, size, x, y, filter?}` → `{nodeId?, sub?}`, using the same framing as `viewport.capture`.
- `viewport.select` accepts `SubRef` strings; `ui.context` adds `sub`, `cplane`, `tool`.
- `design.apply` unchanged (200 ops, `changeId` idempotency). Studio: one `Command` per verb with `keywords: aliases`, group `'Model'`, which calls the same `build`.
- `design.export {format: 'step'|'3dm'}`: plan check (step 8, §4.11).

**Kernel types:** `FeatureOutput.curves?: CurveOut[]`; `Evaluation.curves: CurvePart[]` (`{id, nodeId, rev, polyline, points: {key, at, kind?, mirrored?}[], control?, closed, plane?, snaps}`), sent with the same `rev` skipping as parts; `Part.snaps?: {kind: centre|girdleQuad|culet|prongTip|headBase, at}[]` for stones and settings; `Part.topology?: {faceNames, faceTriStarts, edgeNames, edgeStarts, edgeCrease}` only for nodes in `topologyFor`; `FeatureOutput.host?: StoneHost`, `FeatureOutput.shank?: ShankData`; `EvaluateOptions.topologyFor?: string[]`, `gesture?: boolean`; `Kernel.inspect`.

**Diagnostic codes:** `curve.open` ("Close the curve to extrude it"), `curve.selfIntersects`, `extrude.noLoop`, `sweep.profileMismatch`, `fillet.edgeMissing` (warning), `fillet.rebound` (info), `fillet.partial` (warning, fix), `fillet.invalid` and `fillet.failed` (error, input passed through, fix), `fillet.timeout` (warning: "This round took too long to compute; try a smaller radius", input passed through), `fillet.noEdges`, `shell.nothingRemoved` (error, input passed through), `shell.vent` (warning: "A closed hollow needs a vent hole to cast"), `names.lostInMesh` (blocked), `names.none` (blocked: "This loft has no face names; pick it whole"), `boolean.mesh` (info), `deform.mesh` (info: "a mesh, not in STEP"), `editable.unsupported` (info), `engine.crashed`.

## 4. Hard parts and decisions

1. **One way to model.** Every verb is a node; gestures write ops; parameter handles edit one field. *Rejected:* direct-first write-back as the default (dragging a side face moves the two sketch points that made it). It needs a per-kind mapping the AI must predict, a `design.writeback` second write path, and a `pushPull` node for owner-less faces, for a tool Rhino jewelers use occasionally (MoveFace is frequency C). *Rejected:* separate direct and history modes, and Freeze (history off): nothing in Pavé "breaks history", and slow chains are handled by gesture evaluation (item 6).

2. **Curves as data: one curve per node, keyed points with per-key ranks.** A curve node is one path or loop; holes are `extrude.holes`; joining is `curveOp{join}`. Points are `Record<Key, {at, rank, kind?, w?}>`, so one drag, one insert or one delete is one small patch on one key, and the designer's undo of point *d* (or of inserting *e*) leaves the assistant's edit of point *b* (or insert of *f*) alone. Symmetry is evaluated, not stored: dragging a point is one patch and its mirror follows. Point records must never carry a `type` key and point patches must not touch `shape.type`, because `guardPatch` restores any object with `type` whole or not at all [V]. *Rejected:* an `order: Key[]` array beside the records (the first design; a merge patch replaces arrays whole, so an insert's undo after a concurrent insert left `order` and `points` disagreeing [V]); arrays like `pipe.points` (same reason); a `sketch` node whose curves share points (a small 2D constraint system, harder for the AI to write and to validate); a `patchPoints` op (a sixth op every consumer must learn, for no gain).

3. **Topological naming: four layers, cheapest first, never guess.**
   - *Layer 0, avoid it:* `extrude.roundStart/roundEnd/flare`, sweep caps and profile interpolation, `primitive.fillet`, `vectorBody.bevel` take a radius or a shape and need no names; most jewelry rounding softens a rim or blends a head into shoulders [E]. **The spikes moved the signet's head-to-band round here:** an exact r 0.6 fillet on a fused head and band was not done in 10 of 10 drags and in every case where the head sat on the band rather than in it, even at r 0.1, and failed attempts took up to 7 s (ff-join-radii, ff-s10-drag [V]). No proof in §5 depends on an exact join fillet.
   - *Layer 1, provenance names:* extrude `side:<segmentKey>`, `cap:start`, `cap:end`; sweep and loft one face per profile segment `side:<key>` plus caps; revolve `side:<key>`; primitive `top, bottom, …, wall`. Names are prefixed with the node id, carried through booleans by `Modified`/`Generated`/`IsDeleted` (in the build [V]), with ordinals `#k` for split faces outside the base name. An edge is its two faces' base names, sorted, joined by `|`. Two independent spikes kept every name through a moved point, a taller body, an inserted point and a moved, larger hole [V]. **A boolean also tags its section edges** (`BRepAlgoAPI_Fuse.SectionEdges()`, in the build [V]) as `join:<booleanId>`, carried downstream like any name: the seam between two bodies is a semantic set, not a face-pair pattern. The first design's pattern `ext/side:*|swp/side:*` picked 10 of the 17 seam edges (it missed the head's bottom cap and the band poking through the top), and 10 of 20 at US 7 (ff-s2s3-signet [V]).
   - *Layer 2, selectors as rules:* a view pick stores exact names; chain and loop picks store a pattern or a `join:` tag; patterns pick up new segments (an inserted point *e* added `side:e` to `side:*`), exact names never gain edges.
   - *Layer 3, hints:* `near` stores each pick point. A vanished name re-binds only when exactly one crease edge lies within 0.2 mm and 10° (`fillet.rebound`); two or more candidates, or none, and the edge is reported missing with "Pick again". A wrong fillet in gold is worse than a missing one.
   - Mesh bodies have no names: sub-shape picking is off on them ("This body is a mesh: select it whole"), downstream edge nodes are `blocked` with `names.lostInMesh`, and the advice is the Rhino lore, round before combining (the DAG makes reordering a two-op fix).
   - `ShapeUpgrade_UnifySameDomain` is not run on named bodies: its `History()` throws at runtime in this build ("unbound types: handle<BRepTools_History>", S4 [V]), so coplanar faces stay split (fused signets carry 20–25 faces). *Rejected:* the direct proposal's "merge names by the larger face"; OCAF/TNaming (not in the build, and it puts geometry state in the document); pure geometric matching (silently wrong under a ring's symmetry); index naming (the spike's "edge 5" moved from (6, 2, 0) to (4.5, −0.75, 0) after one insert [V]); geometric queries as stored selectors (`crease > 30°`, `parallel to Z`), deferred until patterns prove short.

4. **Fillet and shell robustness.** Reproduced here [V]: on a 15-edge prism r 0.3 and 0.9 are valid; **r 1.6 returns `IsDone` with an invalid solid whose volume grew from 56.1 to 77.5 mm³**; r 3 is not done; filleting a full revolve's seam edges throws a raw `WebAssembly.Exception`. On sweep creases r 0.1–0.5 work in 41–58 ms and r 0.8 is not done; on fused joins see item 3. Policy, inside `scoped()`/`occtError()`:
   1. Offer and accept only crease edges, computed from **edge regularity**: `BRepLib.EncodeRegularity` then `BRep_Tool.Continuity(edge, f1, f2)` (both in the build [V]), C0 = crease. The first measurement used replicad's `normalAt`, which on station-sweep B-spline faces evaluates the wrong spot and reported smooth profile joins as 11–84° creases (6 "creases" on a band with 2) [V]; robust normals read 0.0° at the smooth joins and 77.7–82.8° at the corners. `describeSub`'s plain words and `design.inspect` normals use the same robust evaluation (pcurve at the edge parameter, face orientation honoured).
   2. Guard every fillet, shell and offset result: `IsDone`, `BRepCheck_Analyzer` valid, finite positive volume, and a volume change consistent with the operation: negative when every edge is convex; for a shell, removed volume within ±30 % [E] of the inner area × thickness, so a shell that removes nothing fails (ff-shell: a rounded heart returned done, valid, 0.000 mm³ removed [V]). A fixed share of the body's volume is not used: correct shells removed 54 % and 62 % [V]. The guard costs about 6 ms [V].
   3. **Fillets run in the exact worker, never in the draft worker**, each attempt under a 300 ms deadline. A WASM call cannot be interrupted, so the scheduler terminates and respawns that worker on overrun (respawn cost measured in S14) and caches `fillet.timeout` for that input hash and radius, so it is not retried on every keystroke. Draft shows the input with "Rounding…" until the result arrives.
   4. Ladder on failure, capped by **wall time (1 s)** rather than by tries: all edges at r; then each edge alone to fillet the good set and warn `fillet.partial` with the failing names; then bisections to attach `fix: "Use 0.42 mm"`. S5 measured the uncapped ladder at p50 231 ms, p95 1.8 s, max 2.5 s, up to 28 attempts [V]; the brief's "about 0.5 s" did not hold.
   5. If nothing works, pass the input through with `fillet.failed` or `fillet.timeout`. Never throw, so dependents are never `blocked` by an ambitious radius.
   6. **Shell by kind:** revolve and the sweep family hollow by offsetting the 2D profile inward and rebuilding (valid in 6 ms on a revolved dome where `MakeThickSolid` threw a raw `WebAssembly.Exception` [V]); `MakeThickSolid` only for prisms and other planar-walled bodies. A shell needs at least one open face: with none, `MakeThickSolid` returned the inner offset solid, not a hollow [V]; `closed: true` builds body minus inward offset and warns `shell.vent`. Shell after a boolean is best effort (the signet opened under its head was not done [V]); the advice is to hollow the parts first.
   7. A per-node fault counter marks a node that faults the WASM runtime twice in a row `engine.crashed`.
   *Rejected:* failing the node outright (it blocks stones and exports downstream), trusting `IsDone` alone (measured above to ship broken castings), and a volume cap as a share of the body (it rejects correct shells).

5. **Selection that survives re-evaluation.** Selections are `SubRef` strings, never triangle indices. Points survive anything but deletion. After each evaluation the workspace re-resolves edges and faces against `Part.topology`; refs that vanished drop out with one live-region notice, and if all go the selection falls back to the node. During a drag the gesture's names are frozen. Sub-shape selection is a draft-detail feature (production fuses parts per metal).

6. **Performance.** Curves never wait for the worker: the running tool and dragged points are drawn on the main thread from `@pave/kernel/curve` (S9: 0.23 ms p95 per frame for 400 points; a periodic control-point re-solve, 3.7 ms p95, is budgeted into the drag frame [V]). Solids catch up through the latest-wins scheduler. Measured on one shared Node core [V]: drawn-profile sweep 23 ms analytic (69 ms exact B-rep on demand), extrude 1–13 ms, naming 13–80 ms, fillet 8–144 ms on prisms and 41–58 ms on sweep creases, a fused-join fillet 0.4–7 s and mostly not done, cached re-evaluation 0.6–1.5 ms; draft drag through a sweep mesh and a Manifold union 9.2/22.3 ms p50/p95; the exact chain (sweep B-rep plus fuse) 203/347 ms; an exact union of a ring shank and a head 2.1 s at production and STEP export of that piece 8.1 s.
   **Exact booleans policy** (the first design never said how a fillet after a Combine works when draft booleans are Manifold meshes, `features/boolean.ts` `prefer: ctx.detail === 'draft' ? 'mesh' : 'exact'` [V]): a boolean, or a node of several solids, is exact at draft **only when a name-consuming node depends on it** (`fillet`, `shell`, `curveOp{edges|section}`); otherwise it stays a mesh. B-rep only when an exact operation downstream asks (fillet, shell, exact boolean, STEP); names only for nodes in `topologyFor` (the selected body in a sub-object filter, or a selector's target). During a gesture (`EvaluateOptions.gesture`), evaluation stops at the first heavy node (fillet, shell, exact boolean, or EWMA over 40 ms), whose last result shows at 50 % with a 1 px progress hairline, and full evaluation runs on release.
   **Targets:** point drag feeding a sweep and a mesh union under 60 ms p95 (met in Node, to be measured in the browser worker, S10); templates keep the CAD plan's 120 ms; **release re-evaluation of an exact chain 0.5–3 s with progress shown**, not 120 ms; fillets and shells arrive asynchronously from the exact worker; every chrome surface open costs under 2 ms of frame time (§5).

7. **Bodies are closed.** Surfaces are construction, or come with a thickness, because casting needs closed metal and every body must be weighable; `checkGeometry` already rejects open shells. NetworkSrf, EdgeSrf and Patch therefore wait for a `surface` node with a built-in thickness.

8. **Non-uniform scale without `GTransform`.** `BRepBuilderAPI_GTransform` is not in the build [V]. On curves the gumball bakes the scale into points or the shape's fields (a circle becomes an ellipse); on bodies a non-uniform handle adds `deform{scale}`, exact for curves and the sweep family (the sections are scaled, then built; within 0.0001 % of the exact affine volume, S7 [V]), Manifold `warp` otherwise with the mesh badge. `base.transform` stays a similarity.

9. **Keys and planes.** `w/e/r` gumball keeps the CAD plan's W/E. Drawing keys are only `c`, `l`, `p`; everything else is one word away in the command bar. Rhino's finish-and-repeat is kept: Enter, Space and right-click finish a command, Enter and Space repeat it, and right-click offers *Repeat* as the first row of the canvas menu, so right-click pans only when dragged and the studio's right-click *Ask AI…* stays. Delete and Backspace act on points while points are being drawn or edited. The plane follows the view preset, so a Rhino user's "Right view means Right CPlane" holds with no new keys; curves drawn over a host attach to it. *Rejected:* `5/6/7/8` plane keys and hover-follows-face (surprising mid-gesture); "Plane to face" is an explicit verb.

10. **Jewelry on drawn bodies.** `BODY_KINDS` makes drawn bodies valid refs for `gem.placement.surface`, `pave.host`, `engraving.host`, `bail.host` and `boolean`, but that alone gives free-standing heads: today `StoneHost` comes only from `ringHost(shankData)`, a surface stone gets `host: null` and `setting: 'flush'`, a prong or bezel head with no host stands on its own base ring, and `gemRow.host`, `placement.ringTop.shank` and inside/outside engraving accept only `ringShank` [V gem.ts, setting.ts, nodes.ts, engraving.ts]. So **step 3** adds:
    - a generic `StoneHost` that any body publishes (a ray cast on its mesh, the way `bail.ts` finds metal), so a prong or bezel head on a drawn band roots its claws in the band's metal;
    - `ShankData` published by a sweep whose rail is a ring curve (rail, stations, inner and outer surfaces), and forwarded by a boolean from its target, so `ringTop`, `gemRow` and inside engraving accept drawn bands;
    - stones and settings publish snap points (centre, girdle quads, culet, prong tips, head base), and `curveOp{outline: gem}` with a plane attached to the stone, so a halo frame, bezel seat or gallery drawn from a stone follows a stone change. With `design.replaceStone`, node mounts follow (as today), curves attached to the stone and outlines of it follow, and curves merely snapped to it do not; the inspector says which.
    - A free stone placed with nothing picked faces the active construction plane's normal (out of the screen), not world +Z, so a stone in a Pendant's Front view faces the viewer.
    Step 6 adds `gemRow.along` (stones on a drawn rail, with graduation), `pave.region.faces`, click-to-place stones on any body, milgrain and galleries as `pattern{curve}` over `curveOp{edges}`, `relief` on a host face, and **"Make editable"** for `ringShank`: one undoable change that writes a rail, profiles and a `sweep` whose width, thickness and centre come as `laws` sampled at the shank's own stations, with the ring-size field still driving the rail, and **rewrites every ref to the shank** (stones, rows, engraving, booleans) to the new body in the same change (`rewriteRefs`). Eight profiles cannot reproduce the template (S8: cathedral −12.3 %, euro −4.8 % [V]); laws at the 96 template stations reproduce plain, tapered, cathedral and euro exactly [V]. A split shank is two strands, so it becomes two sweeps plus a union (−3.9 % from one strand [V]); a variant not within 0.1 % is not offered (`editable.unsupported`).

11. **.3dm, STEP and the paid line.** STEP (paid) is exact for every exact node; mesh fallbacks are left out with today's note; the exact sweep exported in 46 ms, and a drawn ring round-trips valid with 8 faces and equal volume (S12 [V]); Rhino imports STEP natively [S], so **STEP is the recommended exact route into Rhino**. .3dm (paid): rhino3dm 8.35 writes and reads back exact `NurbsCurve`s, untrimmed `NurbsSurface` faces as `Brep`s (periodic surfaces for closed bands, within 7.1e-15 mm of the kernel's surface) and planar caps trimmed by a curve [V], so curves go out exact on a "Construction" layer, sweep-family and extrude faces as untrimmed surfaces with trimmed planar caps, one layer per node; faces with general trims (fillets, boolean results) go out as meshes. rhino3dm cannot join faces (no join, joinBreps, merge or boolean union [V]), so a .3dm body is always loose surfaces; the notes say "faces are separate; Join them in Rhino; rounded and combined faces are meshes in .3dm; STEP has them exact as solids". STEP import is free. No export is gated anywhere today and no plan owns entitlements [V], so step 8 adds one check at `design.export` and the export menu for `step` and `3dm`, reading the plan field the platform work exposes; until it exists the formats stay open and the menu marks them.

12. **The designer and the assistant on one curve.** Different points of one curve edit, insert, delete and undo independently, because every one of those touches one key. An assistant edit during a gesture is handled by the session as today (pending and live gesture re-applied over the new confirmed state). If the assistant deletes a point the designer is dragging, the gesture's patch re-creates a record without a `rank`, `NodeSchema` rejects it, and the existing reject path drops it with a toast.
