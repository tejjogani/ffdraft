# Own the item master, rent the books

_Research date 24 November 2026. **Read this first: every outside fact in this report comes from search-result extracts. No web page could be opened in this session**, so vendor prices, customer counts, legal thresholds and tax rates below were seen only in a search tool's summary of the linked page. Labels: **[S]** search extract, not read at source; **[E]** estimate or inference; **[V: repo]** a first-hand read of the Pavé repository on 24 November 2026 (the only verified items here). No [S] claim is established fact. Check prices and terms with the vendors, and every legal item with an Indian chartered accountant and a US lawyer, before money is committed. This is not legal advice._

The jewelry and diamond trade has no system of record that starts where the piece starts. In every segment searched, from Surat diamond factories to US custom jewelers, the ERP begins after the design is finished, a person retypes the item master, and **no ERP was found that reads a CAD file to fill it** [S]. The state of the art is a fragmented field of small, mostly desktop vendors with almost no independent reviews; migrations done by hand by the incoming vendor, which move item masters and stock but flatten every open memo, repair and workshop balance into closed totals; and no funded AI-native entrant [S, E]. The law falls hardest on the books: India's tamper-proof edit log with eight-year retention and daily India-resident backups, IRP e-invoicing, GST by HSN code, and cash and anti-money-laundering thresholds. Those duties already live in Tally, QuickBooks or the incumbent ERP. A stock-and-production record carries a lighter load, mostly fields and wording: lab-grown terminology, a HUID on each hallmarked piece, the diamond warranty statement on business-to-business documents, origin evidence for stones of 0.5 ct and up, and an unbroken change history with export [S, E]. Pavé can therefore take the vertical from the design outward: make its CAD the place items are born, push that item master and a job package into whatever ERP a customer runs, then hold the made-to-order job through to a tagged piece, while leaving books, tax, payments and loose-stone trading (Diaex's ground) alone. Of the earlier positions, **"never build an ERP" should narrow to "never build the books"**, and **"never build a migration product" should stand as a product but become a deliberate import engine**, built once there is something worth migrating into; the rest of the never-build list holds, with workshop metal accounts and hallmarking changing from "nothing" to "fields and exports". None of this sits on the founder's critical path: the local CAD test with his own Claude API key depends on the model wiring and the engine, not on any ERP work, and four cheap schema choices made now spare a rebuild later.

## Every segment's ERP starts after the design, and none reads the CAD

The trade runs on three disconnected layers in each half of the business. On the diamond side, a planner decides what a rough stone can yield, a factory ERP tracks where each packet is and what it weighs, and trading software pushes stock to marketplaces. On the jewelry side, a CAD tool produces the design, a production ERP or spreadsheet tracks metal and jobs, and an accounting package keeps the books. The handover between layers is a file export or a person retyping [E]. The table summarises who runs each segment; all vendor facts are [S] and self-reported.

### Diamonds split into planner, packet and price list

Rough planning is a hardware-and-software duopoly: Sarine reports **over 25,000 Advisor installations** ([S] [Sarine presentation](https://mayafiles.tase.co.il/rpdf/1577001-1578000/P1577934-00.pdf)) and Lexus/OctoNus scanners start at **€30,000** ex-works ([S] [Lexus](https://lexusindia.in/ap-hr.html)), and their planning files are proprietary enough that a converter vendor sells a way to open them ([S] [reaConverter](https://howto.reaconverter.com/how-to-open-sarine-advisor-adv-and-galaxy-cap-files/)). Factory ERPs are a long tail of Surat software houses selling packet issue and return, lab stones, barcodes and grading through IndiaMART ([S] [Visione](https://www.visioneinfotech.com/diamond-manufacturing-software); [DMFG](https://www.indiamart.com/proddetail/dmfg-diamond-manufacturing-erp-software-20461066591.html)), with SAP S/4HANA at the top ([S] [Wipro case](https://www.wipro.com/applications/case-study/diamond-processing-company-cuts-costs-and-complexities-with-sap-s4hana/)). Lab-grown adds reactor software that stops at the crystal ([S] [Upstair](https://www.upstairtechnologies.com/portfolio/cvd-factory-management-digital-platform-for-diamond-and-engineering-process)) at a scale desktop tools were not built for: Kira runs **4,000 reactors** and makes **250,000+ polished carats a month** ([S] [Solitaire](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/kira-expands-to-4000-reactors-becomes-worlds-largest-lab-grown-diamond-producer/)). Lab-grown wholesale prices fell about **26% on average in 2025**, though the latest quarter fell only 4.7%, the smallest drop yet ([S] [Professional Jeweller](https://www.professionaljeweller.com/lab-grown-diamond-prices-down-in-2025/)); no product found revalues stock against current prices [E].

Trading software is mature and crowded. DiamTrade, in business since 1998, lists **$750 a year cloud and $5,000 desktop** and pushes stock to RapNet, IDEX, Blue Nile and James Allen ([S] [SoftwareSuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/diamtrade)), and marketplaces have moved to APIs: Nivoda takes supplier stock "via API or FTP" ([S] [Nivoda supplier help](https://supplyhelp.nivoda.com/hc/en-gb/articles/35390236532753-How-can-I-integrate-my-inventory-system-with-the-Nivoda-platform)) and offers holds and orders through its Pro API ([S] [Nivoda API guide](https://buyerhelp.nivoda.com/hc/en-gb/articles/47645423678993-API-Installation-Guide)). There is no industry data standard; Rapaport Trade's accepted-headers list, which maps nine spellings of "stock number" to one field, is the de facto dictionary ([S] [Rapaport Trade fields](https://help.rapaport.com/en/articles/5469769-rapaport-trade-formerly-rapnet-diamond-fields-values)). Two facts matter for lab-grown specifically. Rapaport Group adopted an **official policy excluding synthetics on 20 April 2026** ([S] [Business Wire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260420160537/en/Rapaport-Press-Release-Rapaport-Group-Reaffirms-Commitment-to-Natural-Diamonds-and-Announces-Official-Policy-Excluding-Synthetics)), so lab-grown stock syndicates to Nivoda, IDEX and VDB instead. And only GIA has a public certificate API (GraphQL, pay-as-you-go, asset links that expire in 60 minutes) ([S] [GIA Report Results API](https://gia.edu/report-results-api)); no IGI API was found, and IGI grades most lab-grown [E]. This whole layer is the stone record Diaex already holds. It is not Pavé's ground, and nothing in the evidence makes it so.

### Jewelry vendors all keep weight ledgers, and none keeps the CAD

Metal kept as a ledger of its own, in fine grams per party beside the currency balance, is standard in jewelry-specific systems ([S] [Daysum](https://daysum.net/en/jewelry-consignment-software-erp/)), as is the karigar (workshop artisan) pattern: issue metal and stones by weight, receive pieces and scrap, compare loss with a tolerance per process, debit the excess to the worker ([S] [Synergics](https://www.synergicssolutions.com/jewellery-erp-suite/jewellery-manufacturing-software); [FlowSense](https://www.flowsense.solutions/jewellery-manufacturing-erp)). Tally handles outsourced gold work as "job work-out" ([S] [TallyNine](https://tallynine.com/job-work-for-gold-processing/)) but cannot price an ornament from the day's rate and weight, so the usual Indian split is a jewelry ERP for operations feeding Tally for the books ([S] [Whirl Designs](https://whirldesigns.com/blog/jewellery-erp-for-small-jewellers)). The Indian price formula is built into the software: net weight times the day's rate per karat, plus wastage, plus making charge per gram, per piece or as a percentage, plus stones, plus tax ([S] [open-source jewelry ERP](https://github.com/webotapp-academy/jewellery-software)). Billed wastage is partly a pricing convention, not physical loss, so a record has to keep the two apart [E]. Casting trees are grouped and reconciled against a theoretical metal figure by a few vendors ([S] [Jobin & Jismi](https://www.jobinandjismi.com/industries/jewellery-manufacturing); [FlowSense](https://www.flowsense.solutions/jewellery-manufacturing-erp)), and one MES vendor argues ERPs track order status but not bench weights ([S] [iFactory](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/diamond-and-jewellery/why-jewellery-erp-is-not-enough-without-manufacturing-execution-system)).

The gap that matters for Pavé is upstream of all this. No mainstream ERP (PIRO, Valigara, Ornate, the SAP and Business Central add-ons, the Indian vendors) was found importing a CAD file to fill the item master, weights or stone list [S]. MatrixGold's technical report carries a gemstone table and metal weights but exports to PDF ([S] [MatrixGold 4.0](https://en.design-engineering.net/matrixgold/matrixgold-4-0)); one CAD vendor claims an Excel or JSON export "for ERP integration" without describing the mapping ([S] [eLUXE3D](https://eluxe3d.com/integrated-digital-manufacturing-systems-for-jewelry-eluxe3d/)); RhinoArtisan sells production reports with stone and metal weight estimates ([S] [RhinoArtisan](https://www.rhinoartisan.com/manufacturers/)). The closest thing to Pavé's quote is **Diatech Make, a browser CAD with "a stone list with cut, size, count and carats, and one-click pricing from your Studio price book"** ([S] [Diatech Make](https://cad.diatech.ai/features)), but its data stays inside that CAD. From the other direction, Valigara has added jobs, bills of materials and costing to its catalogue product ([S] [Valigara](https://www.valigara.com/company-updates/valigara-adds-jewelry-jobs-production-management-software/)), so the space between design and ERP is being approached from both ends. Nobody visible occupies it yet [E].

### Retail and wholesale are small pools stitched together

US independents mostly run The Edge, which claims **about 3,500 jewelers** ([S] [edgeuser.com](https://edgeuser.com/News/happy-new-year1)), sells a perpetual licence at **$4,600** for one station plus 15–20% a year in support ([S] [CardFellow](https://www.cardfellow.com/blog/the-edge-jewelry-pos-review)), and was **bought by Serent Capital on 27 April 2026** ([S] [Business Wire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260427205866/en/The-Edge-Partners-with-Serent-Capital-and-Appoints-Josh-Brenner-as-CEO)). Its moat is an integration web, above all a two-way sync with Punchmark websites covering products, customers, repairs and transactions ([S] [Punchmark docs](https://punchmark.atlassian.net/wiki/x/BQCITw)). Jewel360 is the cloud challenger at **$299 a month, or $199 billed annually**, with a $750 setup fee rebated on its payments ([S] [Jewel360 pricing](https://jewel360.com/pricing)). An independent's stack of POS, website, ring builder, texting and QuickBooks costs roughly **$8,000–15,000 a year across four or five vendors** [E], and about 17,000 US retail jewelers ([S] [JBT via Solitaire](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/?p=34175)) at $3,000–10,000 each puts the whole US retail software pool near **$50–170M a year** [E]. That is a small pool, and a private-equity owner now consolidating it is a reason not to fight for the POS.

India's feature baseline is richer and cheaper: daily rate pricing, making and wastage, old-gold exchange as tender, HUID on tags and invoices, savings schemes, girvi (gold loans) and karigar job work are advertised by almost every local package ([S] [VelGold](https://velgold.in/); [GOLDBOOK](https://www.goldbook.in/)), and the large chains run Dynamics 365 or Oracle ([S] [Microsoft: Malabar](https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/19175-malabar-gold-and-diamonds-azure-data-lake-analytics); [Business Today: Tanishq](https://businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/oracles-low-code-application-apex-helps-tanishq-cut-tech-support-costs-by-30-and-generate-rs-1000-crore-in-additional-sales-388243-2023-07-05)). Only **15–20% of Indian jewelry manufacturing units are organised** ([S] [World Gold Council](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/indias-gold-jewellery-market-structure)), so spreadsheets and paper are likely the real incumbent at the bottom [E]. Wholesale runs through three pipes that never meet the retailer's POS stock: vendor catalogues onto websites (GemFind JewelCloud, Stuller Showcase), diamond feeds into ring builders, and memo and purchase orders through the vendor's ERP ([S] [Stuller](https://stuller.com/e-commerce-offerings); [Valigara](https://www.valigara.com/main-features/); [E] for the synthesis).

### Nobody funded owns the record, because capital follows liquidity

No funded, verifiably adopted AI-native jewelry or diamond ERP surfaced. The "AI-native" labels belong to unfunded or unverified vendors ([S] [Bonx](https://www.bonx.com/blog/how-to-choose-between-generic-erp-and-jewelry-specific-software)), and the funded AI-native ERPs are financials-first with no manufacturing depth ([S] [ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/en-us/ai-native-erp)). The money went to marketplaces with balance sheets: Nivoda raised a **$51M Series C** in November 2024 and a **$60M credit facility** in September 2025 to give buyers 3- to 90-day terms ([S] [Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/nivoda-snaps-60m-to-enable-flexible-payment-options-for-retailers-and-diamond-buyers/); [Nivoda help](https://buyerhelp.nivoda.com/hc/en-gb/articles/31617022896657-What-invoicing-terms-and-payment-methods-are-available)). Meanwhile Sarine's revenue fell about 24.5% in 2025 as lab-grown took share ([S] [Rapaport](https://rapaport.com/news/sarine-revenue-drops-amid-low-demand-for-natural-diamonds/)). The opening is real, and it is narrower than "be the ERP".

## Migration moves the masters and flattens everything still open

Migration in this trade is a service the incoming vendor performs, not a product anyone sells. The Edge's documented process is the most complete found: a signed conversion-standards form, a converted "preliminary database" the jeweler checks, then go-live, with **open repairs converted as final sales "that must be voided and re-entered manually"** and open layaways likewise ([S] [Edge Conversion Standards](https://devuser.edgeuser.com/Knowledge/Knowledge-Base/conversion-conversion-procedure-initial-phase-conversion-standards)). Competitors quote **1–3 weeks** for a single store from CSV, Excel, Visual FoxPro files, SQL backups or QuickBooks ([S] [WJewel](https://www.wjewel.com/blog/how-to-switch-jewelry-pos-system.php)) and **3–4 weeks** typically ([S] [Bravo](https://www.bravostoresystems.com/post/best-jewelry-store-pos-software-in-2026)). Indian vendors offer free setup of items, parties and opening stock from Tally or Excel in a test environment ([S] [Ornexa](https://ornexajewelleryerp.in/blog/best-jewellery-software-india-buyers-guide)), one sells a layer that syncs with Tally rather than replacing it, holding scheme liabilities and karigar metal ledgers that Tally lacks ([S] [Eriont](https://eriont.com/jewellery-software/accounting/)), and one vendor says plainly that "preparing data for import cannot be the responsibility of the ERP solution provider" ([S] [GoPiro](https://www.gopiro.com/blog/are-you-implementing-new-jewelry-erp-thing-definitely-causing-you-headache)). No vendor publicly describes carrying open memos, karigar metal balances or gold-scheme instalments over as live documents, and those are where the money and the legal exposure sit [E].

Proof of a correct cut-over is thin. The Edge compares the number of customers, in-stock items and vendors, and the **total cost of in-stock inventory**, against the old system's reports ([S] [Edge QA checklist](https://edgeuser.com/Knowledge/Knowledge-Base/conversion-conversion-qa-checklist-general-data-verification)). Nothing found reconciles grams, fine weight or carats. The earlier Pavé rule, that rows, carats, grams and value must equal the source, is therefore **ahead of documented practice**, not merely adequate. A trade-grade proof would add, per location and per status (own stock, memo in, memo out, at karigar): tag count, gross grams, net metal by purity and fine weight, stone carats and pieces, certificate count with numbers re-checked against the lab, cost and retail, and open jobs [E]. GIA lookups are reported at about **$0.20 each** ([S] [GIA](https://gia.edu/report-results-api)); lab-grown certificates from IGI have no API path found.

The data sits in Tally (reachable over XML on port 9000 or ODBC, with a known pitfall where duplicate voucher IDs overwrite existing vouchers on import) ([S] [Tally help](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/)), in SQL Server or Access databases behind desktop ERPs, in FoxPro files, in report screens printed to CSV, and in paper books [S, E]. Ornate, one of the larger Indian packages, documents no export path and is listed as having no API ([S] [SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/ornate)). The Edge has a self-service item import whose column headers must match its own field names ([S] [Edge item import](https://edgeuser.com/Knowledge/Knowledge-Base/inventory-item-data-importexport)), which matters in the other direction too: it is a ready target for an item master exported from Pavé.

AI helps with mapping but has not removed the human. On hard benchmarks, **self-improving LLM matching reaches 62.2% top-1 accuracy** on a clinical schema pair ([S] [Matchmaker](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.24105)) and plain GPT-3.5 scored a **mean F1 of 0.241** ([S] [arXiv 2508.15610](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15610)); the commercial importers publish no accuracy figures, and Flatfile's automatic mapping aborts the whole job if a single column falls below its confidence threshold ([S] [Flatfile Automap](https://flatfile.com/docs/plugins/automap)). Microsoft's AI migration tool is in public preview for CRM, with ERP scenarios planned ([S] [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/microsoft-launches-dynamics-365-activate-to-accelerate-ai-powered-crm-and-erp-migrations/)). No benchmark exists for handwritten ledgers of weights and purities. Jewelry schemas are small and regular, so **85–95% of columns mapped automatically** on a new legacy source is a fair planning figure, with a person still confirming net against gross weight, cost against tag price, billed wastage against loss, whether each memo is still open, and the final control totals [E].

Other verticals show what makes migration a weapon. Clio's own team migrates "at no additional cost" for common systems and its "Break Free" offer credited up to six months against an incumbent's remaining contract ([S] [Clio](https://www.clio.com/en-au/data-migration/); [Clio Break Free](https://www.clio.com/web/break-free/)); Shopify bundles free migration by a POS expert into its yearly POS Pro offer ([S] [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/in/pos/pos-pro-yearly)). The pattern is consistent: **the vendor does the work, builds importers for the two or three dominant incumbents, and pays for it out of recurring revenue** [E]. Migration is a cost of acquiring a customer onto a record worth living in. Until Pavé holds such a record, there is nothing to migrate people into.

## The law binds the books hardest and a production record lightly

What a system of record must do to be lawful depends on what it records and what it issues. Three triggers carry almost all the weight: **keeping books of account, issuing tax invoices, and receiving money**. A record of designs, stones, jobs and tagged pieces that does none of the three carries a much lighter load. Read down the last column and a bright line appears. **The expensive, legally sensitive builds (the Indian edit log with India-resident backups, IRP integration, cash aggregation and AML extracts, full stone lineage) all attach to books, invoices, cash or rough stones.** A record that stops short of posting to the books and taking money "may not" inherit the Indian edit-log duty, though it then relies on Tally's [E]; a chartered accountant must confirm where that line falls before Pavé holds any Indian company's stock values. What remains for Pavé is cheap and mostly immediate: generated lab-grown wording, origin and certificate fields on every stone, fineness and marks per metal component, a HUID slot on each tagged piece, and an append-only history with full export. That history earns its keep without any statute. Disputes over cast weight and remakes are settled with it, and the RBI's amended Gold Metal Loan scheme, which from 1 April 2026 lets non-manufacturing jewelers borrow to pay for outsourced manufacturing, requires banks to monitor end use ([S] [RBI](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/press-releases/rbi-issues-amendments-to-gold-metal-loan-gml-scheme)), which is exactly the job-work evidence such a history holds [E].

The first legal obligation therefore arrives with the first PDF, not with any ERP. The pilot's hand-priced sheet goes to buyers in the US and possibly India; "laboratory-grown diamond" is the one phrase found acceptable under both regimes, and "LGD", the trade's own shorthand, is disallowed in India [S]. The earlier memo's open question on cross-border cost also moved: US duties on Indian goods rose from 25% to 50% in 2025, a February 2026 framework was expected to remove duties on natural diamonds and gems, and a Supreme Court ruling against the tariffs reopened the uncertainty ([S] [Rapaport](https://rapaport.com/magazine-article/uncertainty-is-the-new-diamond-world-order/); [Solitaire](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/gem-india-us-trade-agreement-framework-brings-relief-and-sets-stage-for-recovery-gjepc/)). Nothing found covers finished lab-grown jewelry specifically. Before money is committed, five items need checking at source: the CBIC rate for lab-grown diamonds; whether IS 19469 is mandatory under a quality control order; where Rule 3(1) stops for a stock-only system; the US dealer AML thresholds in 31 CFR 1027; and the current duty on India-made lab-grown jewelry entering the US.

## Pavé wins from the design outward, through other people's ERPs

Investors locate the moat in vertical software at the "control point", the application a market cannot do without, and find the highest retention where it is fintech or the back office ([S] [Tidemark](https://www.tidemarkcap.com/vskp-chapter/control-points-patterns-2024)). In jewelry, the back office (books, GST, edit log) belongs to Tally and QuickBooks and is regulated; the front office (US retail POS) belongs to a PE-owned incumbent and a cloud challenger, and in India to cheap local packages; supply belongs to marketplaces; identity and price references belong to GIA and Rapaport [E, from the evidence above]. **The unclaimed control point is the item master and the production record**, the moment a piece is defined and the path from that definition to a tagged, weighed piece. CAD holds it today, but its data does not flow. Tidemark's later idea of an "industry ledger", a record an ecosystem reads rather than one firm ([S] [Tidemark](https://tidemarkcap.com/tools/becoming-an-industry-ledger)), describes the ambition: every ERP downstream reads Pavé's definition of the piece.

The fintech lesson cuts the other way for Pavé. At scale, vertical winners earn most from money moving through the software: **about 82% of Toast's revenue is fintech** ([S] [Tiger/Reuters](https://www.itiger.com/hans/news/2612421472)) and **76% of Shopify's is merchant solutions** ([S] [Shopify 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1594805/000159480526000007/shop-20251231.htm)), while Veeva, where money does not flow through the software, is **84% subscription** ([S] [Veeva](https://ir.veeva.com/news/news-details/2026/Veeva-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2026-Results/default.aspx)). In this trade the money flows in B2B stone and metal purchases on memo and net terms, where bank credit has retreated ([S] [Rapaport](https://rapaport.com/news/banking-on-diamonds/)) and Nivoda is filling the gap with its own credit. That pool sits next to Diaex's stone record, not inside a jewelry ERP, and Diaex's Buy is closed and its Terms say it does not process payment (earlier memo). **Pavé's own model is the Veeva shape (subscription, usage and a per-order fee), and any fintech future runs through Diaex's stone flow**, later and as a separate decision [E].

The AI-native lesson favours Pavé directly. The AI-first entrants that took share sold against labour budgets, not IT budgets, and charged for finished work ([S] [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/assets/uploads/2026/01/BUILDING-VERTICAL-AI_PDF_BESSEMER_VENTURE_PARTNERS_BOOK_JANUARY_2026.pdf); [Primary](https://www.primary.vc/articles/vertical-ai-isnt-vertical-software-with-a-chatbot)). In jewelry the labour budgets are the CAD designer, the clerk who types item masters and uploads stock, and the person who prices a made-to-order quote. Pavé replaces the first and, by computing the bill of materials, removes the second and third. An ERP subscription competes with ₹10,500 Indian billing tools and The Edge's perpetual licence; a design that becomes the item master competes with a salary.

The order of capture follows from that, segment by segment.

| Order | Who | What Pavé holds | What stays in their system | Why here [E] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Made-to-order makers: custom and bench jewelers, small manufacturers and CAD desks, US and India | Design, bill of materials, weights, stone schedule, quote, production package | Books, stock, POS, metal ledger | Sells against CAD labour; no ERP to displace |
| 2 | Stone holders moving downstream, starting with Aska in the pilot | The same, plus stones from Diaex | Their ERP (Heera for Aska), read-only | India's lab-grown studded jewelry exports grew 31% to $1.43B in FY26 while loose polished lab-grown fell 10.6% ([S] [Diamond World](https://www.diamondworld.net/news/indias-gem-and-jewellery-exports-reach-2772-billion-in-fy26)) |
| 3 | Manufacturers and wholesalers on an ERP (PIRO, Valigara, Ornate, Tally plus an Indian ERP) | The item master born in Pavé and pushed by file; the job from design to tagged piece with expected against actual weight | Books, GST, karigar metal ledger, memo documents | The CAD-to-ERP gap is open in every vendor found |
| 4 | Retailers with a custom counter | Quote and design; the finished item pushed into The Edge, Jewel360 or Shopify | POS, repairs, clienteling | The POS pool is small and PE-consolidated; integrate, never replace |
| 5 | Diamond factories and loose-stone traders | Nothing | Planner, factory ERP, trading tool; Diaex if anyone | A different record with different physics |

US and India split the work rather than compete for first place. US manufacturers pay cloud prices (PIRO $299–999 a month ([S] [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/236081/PIRO-Fusion/))), and PIRO's worst reviews complain that basic promises went unfulfilled and standard functions were billed as custom work ([S] [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/retail/piro-fusion-profile/)), so a fixed-scope item-master feed has an opening there. India is where the factory, the karigar and the pricing convention are, so Pavé's quote must speak the Indian formula and its job package must land in a karigar workflow, even when the paying customer is American [E]. The pilot already straddles both, through Aska's US and Indian companies.

## Build nothing ERP-shaped before the CAD test passes

The founder's immediate priority, testing the CAD product locally with his own Claude API key, depends on none of the ERP work. Its critical path is the model wiring and the engine: `packages/agent` has no source files in either checkout today [V: repo], the earlier memo found the server's service file holding interfaces only, and "anthropic" appears in the repository only as a provider value in a server test and a studio fixture [V: repo]. What the ERP path needs from the CAD test is only that it produces correct numbers, namely the metal weight per alloy, the stone schedule and the cost, and that one cast piece is later weighed against them. A caster's own arithmetic makes that check concrete: wax tree weight plus about 10% for the button, times the alloy's specific gravity (13.07 for 14K yellow in Rio Grande's example) ([S] [Rio Grande](https://www.riogrande.com/globalassets/knowledge-hub/instruction-sheets/2023/how-to-determine-metal-weight-for-wax-trees-pdf.pdf)). The difference between Pavé's weight and the cast weight is the same "expected against actual" figure that Indian ERPs reconcile workshop loss against, which is why it matters later [E].

Four choices are cheap now and expensive to retrofit, each about a day to a week of work [E]. Every stone row carries an origin type (natural, laboratory-grown, simulant), a growth method (CVD, HPHT), treatments, the grading lab and the report number. Every displayed stone name is generated from those fields per market, so no quote can ever say "LGD". The rate card accepts both conventions: Western bill of materials plus labour, and the Indian net weight times the day's karat rate plus making (per gram, per piece or percentage) plus wastage, with billed wastage stored apart from measured loss. And every style and variant keeps a stable identifier and an append-only history that is never rewritten, because that history becomes the job record, the dispute record and the export later. Everything else waits, in this order, each step gated on evidence from the one before.

## Conclusion

The asset worth owning is not a ledger but the expected column. Every jewelry ERP found reconciles actual against expected (cast metal against theoretical, karigar returns against issue, stock against tags), and every one of them gets "expected" by having a person retype a CAD report. A design suite that supplies those theoretical numbers per alloy, per stone and per job becomes the input every incumbent depends on without displacing any of them. That is a sharper position than "design-to-order", and it is why the first ERP-shaped build is an export, not a screen.

The legal map gives Pavé a bright line rather than a burden: never post to books, never issue a tax invoice, never take money, and the costly Indian and US duties never attach. Lawfulness then starts with the words on the first PDF sent to a buyer. And because the trade's money moves in stones and credit, which sit beside Diaex rather than inside any ERP, the combined company's path runs from design (Pavé) to stone intent (Diaex), with credit as a later and separate question. Each step is worth taking only after the one before has produced orders, and none of them stands between the founder and a working local CAD test today.
