## 1. US independent and chain retailers: which systems, market share, prices, deployment, integrations

### Takeaway
US independents still mostly run on The Edge (Abbott Jewelry Systems), an on-premise Windows system that claims about 3,500 jewelers. Since 2022 the visible challenger has been cloud-native Jewel360, at $199 to $299 a month. Older desktop systems (ARMS, JewelMate, wJewel), generic POS (Lightspeed, Shopify POS, Square) and many small cloud entrants make up the rest. No independent market-share data exists. Large chains (Signet) run in-house or enterprise systems.

### Inferences
- [E] Market structure among US independents: one dominant on-premise incumbent (The Edge, at most a few thousand stores), one funded cloud challenger (Jewel360, probably hundreds of stores given 144% growth from a 2022 start), and a long tail. Software Advice lists 47 jewelry POS products.
- [E] The typical independent's software spend:
  - POS: Edge licence about $4.6k plus 20% a year, or about $2.4k–3.6k a year on Jewel360.
  - Website: Punchmark or Thinkspace, about $2.4k–3.6k a year.
  - Ring builder: GemFind, about $2.3k–3.5k a year.
  - Clienteling and texting: Clientbook or Podium.
  - Accounting: QuickBooks.

  That is roughly $8k–$15k a year across four or five vendors stitched together by sync software. A new system of record would be priced against that whole bundle, not against the POS line alone.
- [E] The Edge's moat is its integration web (Punchmark, Clientbook, Podium, QuickBooks, Geller repair pricing) and years of data, more than its user experience.

## 2. Jewelry-specific retail functions and how well each system handles them

### Takeaway
Purpose-built systems (The Edge, Jewel360, wJewel, ARMS, JewelMate, PIRO, Pursuit in the UK) all claim the jewelry-specific functions: repairs and job envelopes, custom orders, memo and consignment, trade-ins and scrap, layaway, appraisals, serialised items, multi-store. Generic POS (Lightspeed, Shopify POS) covers serial numbers and basic service orders, and needs add-on apps for the rest. Depth of each function could not be verified, because all evidence is vendor or competitor marketing.

### Inferences

- [E] The functions where generic POS breaks are where state lives outside the sale:
  - a repair or custom job with stages, photos, deposits and a vendor or bench hand-off;
  - memo stock that is not owned;
  - trade-in value applied as tender.

  A new system of record must model these as first-class objects, not as SKUs with notes.
- [E] For Shopify-first jewelers, the stack is fragmented: one app each for repairs, metal pricing, ring building and diamond feeds, and none for memo. This is a clear opening.

## 3. India and the Gulf: retail ERPs for jewellery chains and independents

### Takeaway
India's independent jewellers use a crowded field of local, mostly desktop or hybrid, GST-ready packages. Ornate (about 1,500+ jewellers, self-reported), WinGold, Sun FACETS, GOLDBOOK, VelGold, DataCare, JewelEntry, Komet, SoniERP and Jwelly all advertise the same core set:
- daily gold-rate pricing;
- making and wastage charges;
- old-gold exchange (URD) against the bill;
- HUID on tags and invoices;
- gold savings schemes;
- girvi (gold loans);
- karigar (artisan) job work.

Large chains run enterprise ERP: Malabar, Senco and Joyalukkas on Microsoft Dynamics 365; Tanishq on Oracle, with custom APEX apps; CaratLane on Yantra. Gulf chains use Dynamics or SAP, with jewellery vendors such as Suntech (Sun FACETS) handling UAE VAT and ZATCA.

- [E] India's feature baseline is far richer than the US one. A system without the following cannot sell in India, and almost every local vendor already has them:

## 4. Wholesale and B2B: how manufacturers sell to retailers, virtual inventory and drop-ship

### Takeaway
Manufacturer-to-retailer selling runs through:
- vendor-data networks that push catalogs onto retailer websites (GemFind JewelCloud, Punchmark's vendor program, Stuller Showcase);
- diamond marketplaces with virtual inventory and white-label showrooms (Nivoda: 1.6M+ stones);
- multichannel and B2B tools (Valigara);
- traditional memo programmes run in wholesale ERPs (JewelShop/Acumatica, JewelMate, Diaspark, e-Jewelry);
- general marketplaces such as Faire for fashion and semi-fine.

Drop-ship is common (Stuller Pack & Ship) but is glued together by third-party connectors (Spark Shipping, OrderDesk).

### Inferences
What a new system of record must match or beat:
- [E] Match these, in both regions:
  - jewelry-native objects: repair and custom jobs with stages, photos, deposits and Geller-style price books; memo in and memo out; trade-in and scrap as tender; layaway; appraisals; serialised items with certificates (GIA lookup);

- [E] Beat on:
  - cloud with offline-tolerant POS, and no server in the back room;
  - one ledger for owned, memo and virtual (Nivoda-type supplier-backed) stock;
  - built-in website, ring builder and diamond feeds instead of three vendors;
  - painless migration from The Edge, ARMS and Ornate data. Migration pain is the most-cited switching barrier;
  - transparent monthly pricing, against The Edge's perpetual licence plus 20% maintenance;
  - AI over the unified data: clienteling follow-ups, repair status messages, rate-aware quoting.
- [E] No PE roll-up was found. The market is fragmented into many small, often founder-owned vendors (Punchmark under 49 staff; The Edge claims about 3,500 stores). That makes acquisition of a customer base, for example a website platform, a plausible go-to-market route, and also an attractive target for future roll-ups.
