## 1. The vertical-SaaS playbook as it actually played out: wedge, system of record, multiple products, payments and networks, with figures from filings

### Takeaway
At scale, the winners in vertical software earn most of their revenue from payments and fintech, not from subscriptions:
- **Toast:** about 82% of revenue is fintech.
- **Shopify:** 76% of revenue is "merchant solutions".
- **Mindbody:** about 35% of revenue came from payments, even in 2018.

Subscription is the hook, and payments are the profit engine. The exceptions are verticals where money does not flow through the software:
- **Veeva:** 84% subscription.
- **Procore:** construction payments are still small.
- **ServiceTitan:** about 25% "usage" revenue on $82B of transaction volume.

Investors (Tidemark, a16z, Matrix) put the moat in owning the "control point" or system of record. They see fintech and back-office control points as the ones that retain customers best.

**What investors and analysts say makes a vertical capturable**
- **Tidemark, "control points":**
  - A control point is "the most important application in your software suite". A typical market has one or two: a front-office one that touches the customer and drives sales, and a back-office one.
  - A control point holds some mix of workflow gravity, data gravity and account gravity.
  - Its 2024 benchmark found that companies whose control point is fintech or the back office had the **highest gross and net retention**.
  - Later Tidemark essays describe an "industry ledger" (a system of record for a whole ecosystem, not one firm) and a "system of action", where AI agents act on the data.

  [S] ([Tidemark VSKP truisms](https://www.tidemarkcap.com/vskp-chapter/vertical-saas-truisms); [Control Points 2024](https://www.tidemarkcap.com/vskp-chapter/control-points-patterns-2024); [Industry ledger](https://tidemarkcap.com/tools/becoming-an-industry-ledger)). One Tidemark chapter title argues for "10x your take rate" through "formation and access" [S] ([Tidemark](https://www.tidemarkcap.com/vskp-chapter/formation-and-access--10x-your-take-rate)).
- **a16z:**
  - Fintech raises revenue per customer by **2–5x** (2020, older than 2024).
  - AI could raise it by **2–10x** (September 2024), by taking on work that was too complex for software. This is a forecast, not measured data.
  - The two figures should not be added together.

  [S] ([a16z 2020](https://a16z.com/fintech-scales-vertical-saas/); [a16z 2024](https://a16z.com/vertical-saas-now-with-ai-inside/))
- **Matt Brown (Matrix), "Invisible asymptotes":**
  - Vertical companies bundle payments and try to become the vertical's system of record.
  - Vertical payment take rates are roughly **0.5–1.0% of incoming payments**.
  - "Payments is the beachhead" of the fintech strategy, and the end state is a "vertical ERP".
  - His 2024 data found that fintech adoption in vertical software was "relatively low".
  - A Sacra summary says about 80% of vertical software companies offer embedded payments. The two adoption figures conflict, and the methods are unknown.

  [S] ([notes.mtb.xyz](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/invisible-asymptotes-vertical-software); [fintech adoption](https://notes.mtb.xyz/p/fintech-adoption-vertical-software))
- **Bessemer, "Ten lessons from a decade of vertical software investing"** (2020, older than 2024): it names B2B payments and spend management as a major emerging opportunity. The full text of the lessons could not be retrieved [S] ([BVP](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/ten-lessons-from-a-decade-of-vertical-software-investing)).
- **CRV:** adding payments to vertical SaaS is harder than it looks [S] ([CRV](https://medium.com/crv-insights/vertical-saas-decoded-five-takeaways-investors-and-entrepreneurs-often-overlook-63b3afb50def)).
- **Euclid Ventures:** it invests only in vertical software and vertical AI. Its essays include "The 5 Business Models of Vertical Software" and "The State of Vertical AI". No "capturable vertical" framework from Euclid was found, and the essays could not be read [S] ([Employbl profile](https://www.employbl.com/companies/Euclid)).

### Inferences
- **[E] Revenue mix arithmetic from the figures above:**
  - **Toast:** fintech 82%, subscription 15%, hardware 3% of revenue. ARR per location is about $2.0B / 164k ≈ **$12.5k a year**.
  - **Shopify:** merchant-solutions revenue is about 2.3% of GMV ($8.8B / $378B).
  - **ServiceTitan:** revenue is about 1.2% of GTV ($961M / $82.1B). Usage revenue is about 25% of revenue in Q2 FY27.
  - **Procore:** about $74k average revenue per customer ($1.323B / 17,850).
  - **Veeva:** 84% subscription.
- **[E] Background knowledge from before this session, not verified here:** the time each took to become the system of record.
  - Toast: founded 2011, IPO 2021.
  - Veeva: founded 2007 on Salesforce. It spent about 15 years renting its platform before building Vault CRM, and the switch costs it a multi-year migration.
  - ServiceTitan: founded 2012, IPO December 2024.

  The usual pattern is 7–10+ years from wedge to undisputed system of record.
- **[E] What failed or nearly failed:**
  - **Mindbody:** a weak public valuation led to a PE take-private, and the network value came by buying ClassPass, not by building it. This is evidence that a system of record alone did not create the network effect.
  - **Veeva:** being dependent on a horizontal platform (Salesforce) became a strategic liability.
  - **Toast:** hardware sold at a gross loss shows that the wedge is subsidised.
- **[E] What it means for jewelry and diamonds.** Payments become the profit pool only where money flows through the software. In jewelry and diamonds the large flows are:
  1. B2B stone and metal purchases between dealers, manufacturers and retailers, with memo and net terms;
  2. consumer purchases at retail, with very high tickets and financing;
  3. gold and metal financing.

  A trade marketplace that sits in the stone flow (as Nivoda does) is structurally closer to the Toast and Shopify model than a retail POS like The Edge is.

## 2. Embedded fintech in jewelry and diamonds: who offers it today, and at what take rates

### Takeaway
The trade already runs on credit: memo (consignment), net terms and gold loans. That credit is mostly provided by suppliers or by a shrinking pool of banks, not by software.
- **The marketplaces:**
  - **Nivoda** finances buyers itself through "Nivoda Capital", with terms from 3 to 90 days and a 1.75% surcharge on some options, backed by a $60M credit facility.
  - **IDEX** offers financing too.
- **The insurer:** Jewelers Mutual is building a network of marketplace, financing and insurance around retailers.
- **Consumer financing:** dominated by Synchrony, with more than 4,000 jewelry retailers.
- **Card processing:** jewelers are treated as high-risk, high-ticket merchants.

### Cited Findings
- **Nivoda Capital (B2B net terms):**
  - Buyers can choose terms of **3, 15, 30, 45, 60 or 90 days**. A **1.75% surcharge** appears on some payment options. Credit needs approval, and for US businesses the Jewelers Board of Trade rating may affect the limit [S] ([Nivoda help centre](https://buyerhelp.nivoda.com/hc/en-gb/articles/31617022896657-What-invoicing-terms-and-payment-methods-are-available); [What is Nivoda Capital](https://buyerhelp.nivoda.com/hc/en-gb/articles/30884524921617-What-is-Nivoda-Capital)).
  - Suppliers are paid a few days after the goods reach Nivoda [S] ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=1416479)).
  - In September 2025 Nivoda raised a **$60M financing facility from i80 Group** [S] ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/nivoda-snaps-60m-to-enable-flexible-payment-options-for-retailers-and-diamond-buyers/)).
  - The UK lenders MarketFinance and Kriya list Nivoda as a customer, which suggests trade-credit partners [S] ([MarketFinance](https://marketfinance.com/customer-stories/nivoda); [Kriya](https://kriya.co/customer-stories/nivoda)).
- **IDEX Financing** (2024): launched with a platform upgrade. It covers inventory and also general business needs such as renovations [S] ([AGS](https://www.americangemsociety.org/idex-online-announces-platform-upgrade-financing-option)).
- **Memo terms:** a wholesaler quoted by Rapaport says "Our typical terms for a singular short term memo are net 30" [S] ([Rapaport](https://rapaport.com/magazine-article/not-all-memos-are-created-equal-consignments-various-terms-and-conditions/)).
- **Bank credit to the midstream** (all older than 2024):
  - Antwerp Diamond Bank, Standard Chartered and Bank Leumi left the sector, taking out more than $2B of credit.
  - Union Bank of India and ICICI closed their Antwerp branches in 2018–19, after the Nirav Modi fraud of about $2B.
  - Bain, as cited by Rapaport, put bank credit at about **$8B, down from about $13B**.
  - Four banks (ABN AMRO, Antwerp Diamond Bank, SBI and Standard Chartered) once provided 70% of the industry's financing.
  - In the US only 20–30% of midstream financing comes from banks, against up to 70% in Europe.

  [S] ([Rapaport, Banking on Diamonds](https://rapaport.com/news/banking-on-diamonds/); [Bloomberg via Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/604575/indian-bank-quits-antwerp-diamond-hub-in-blow-for-gem-financing); [National Jeweler](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/5504-another-bank-pulls-back-from-the-diamond-trade))
- **Gold Metal Loans in India:**
  - The RBI issued draft Gold Metal Loan Directions on 29 September 2025 and finalised amendments on **4 December 2025**.
  - From **1 April 2026**, jewellers who are not manufacturers can borrow under the scheme to pay for outsourced manufacturing.
  - The draft proposed raising the tenor for domestic manufacturers from 180 to 270 days. For non-manufacturers the lending bank sets the tenor.
  - Banks must monitor what the gold is used for: it must not be sold or exported in raw form.
  - No interest rates were found.

  [S] ([RBI press release](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/press-releases/rbi-issues-amendments-to-gold-metal-loan-gml-scheme); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/rbi-expands-gold-metal-loan-access-for-jewellers-tightens-use-and-monitoring-norms-505157-2025-12-05))
- **Consumer financing at US jewelers:**
  - Synchrony's Luxury Credit Card is used by **more than 4,000 jewelry retailers** (company-reported).
  - In May 2025 Synchrony and Jewelers Mutual agreed that Jewelers Mutual would promote Synchrony financing in its marketing and in **Zing Marketplace**, its platform for member retailers.

  [S] ([Synchrony IR](https://investors.synchrony.com/news-events/financial-news/detail/521/synchrony-and-jewelers-mutual-collaborate-on-innovative-new-sponsorship-agreement-combining-finance-and-insurance-marketing-efforts); [Synchrony jewelry](https://www.synchrony.com/business/b2b/industries/jewelry))

- **Card processing for jewelers:**
  - Processors treat jewelers as high-risk because of authenticity disputes and high average tickets.
  - A merchant-services vendor quotes large-ticket rates of **1.30–2.70%**.
  - Vendors recommend interchange-plus pricing and Level 2/3 card data.

- **Jewelers Mutual Diamond Marketplace:** launched in 2021, it was upgraded in December 2025 and lists more than **1 million** authenticated natural and lab-grown diamonds. An insurer is running a marketplace [S] ([IDEX Online](https://www.idexonline.com/FullArticle?Id=50037) and [AGS](https://www.americangemsociety.org/?p=48557), as summarised in search).

### Inferences
- **[E] How each lever maps onto the trade:**

  | Lever | Who holds it today | Opening for a software platform |
  |---|---|---|
  | **B2B payments and escrow** | Wire transfers between dealers, on trust | Low fees per transaction (B2B wires). A platform could take about 0.5–1.75% if it adds guaranteed delivery and credit, as Nivoda does. |
  | **Net terms and trade credit** | Proven by Nivoda Capital (surcharge up to 1.75% on some options) | Needs a balance sheet or a credit partner (i80, MarketFinance or Kriya). A software-only entrant would have to partner. |
  | **Memo financing and insurance** | Supplier credit plus insurers (Jewelers Mutual, Jewelers Block) | A system that tracks every stone on memo is the data needed to underwrite memo insurance and memo-backed loans. This is a classic "control point leads to fintech" move. |
  | **Gold Metal Loans and inventory loans (India)** | Banks | The RBI rules require end-use monitoring and MIS reporting. A jeweller's ERP that holds the metal ledger and the karigar (outsourced craftsman) job-work records could become the compliance layer banks rely on. |
  | **Consumer financing** | Synchrony. Jewelers Mutual already bundles it with insurance and a marketplace. | Synchrony looks like the incumbent. |
  | **Card processing** | Processors pricing jewelers as high-risk | High tickets mean basis points matter. A retail POS with built-in payments (Toast-style) could earn about 0.3–0.7% net of interchange on high-value tickets. |

- **[E] The memo and gold-loan rows are my inference, not something found in a source.**
- **[E] The bank retreat** (from about $13B to about $8B, older figures) left a credit gap. The marketplaces are filling it with private credit, which is the "embedded lending" lever already in action.

## 3. Who is trying to own parts of this vertical: scale, strategy, funding and deals 2022–2026

### Takeaway
No one owns the jewelry and diamond system of record.
- **Marketplaces:** the capital is going here. Nivoda has raised about $96M in equity and has a $60M credit facility. Rapaport, VDB, IDEX and the Jewelers Mutual marketplace are the incumbents.
- **Retail POS and ERP:** fragmented, and private equity is starting to consolidate it. Serent Capital bought The Edge in April 2026.
- **Traceability:** under pressure. Sarine's revenue fell 25% in 2025. GIA took 30% of Tracr in June 2026. De Beers itself is being sold for about $1B.
- **CAD:** Stuller-owned Gemvision is the incumbent. A wave of small AI-design startups (BLNG, Gemist, Arcade, Skyveil) raised seed and Series A money in 2025.
- **India:** the software is local billing and ERP (Tally, Marg and many small vendors). The venture money goes into D2C brands, not trade software.

## 4. Market size and structure: businesses by segment, trade volumes, lab-grown share and prices, and what each segment pays for software

### Takeaway
The trade is large but shrinking in natural diamonds, and the business base is fragmenting.
- **India's exports:** $27.72B in FY2025-26, down 3.3%. Cut-and-polished diamond exports fell 8.5% to $12.16B, described as about a 20-year low.
- **Lab-grown:**
  - loose lab-grown exports fell 10.6% in value as prices fell, while volumes rose;
  - lab-grown studded jewellery grew 31%;
  - lab-grown stones are now in 51–61% of US engagement rings.
- **US retailers:** about 16,900 retail jewelers by JBT's count, down 13% since 2020.
- **Surat:** roughly 6–11 lakh (600,000–1.1M) cutting and polishing workers and 7,000+ companies, in crisis after the 2025 US tariffs.
- **Willingness to pay for software** is poorly documented. The one hard price point is CAD at about $3–6k per seat (2021).

### Inferences
- **[E] Retail POS market size.** About 17k US retail jewelers × $3–10k a year of software spend gives roughly **$50–170M a year** for US independent retail POS and ERP. - **[E] The payments pool is much bigger than the software pool.** US jewelry retail sales run at tens of billions of dollars a year (not sourced this session). 0.3–0.7% net of interchange on that is several hundred million dollars a year.
- **[E] The B2B stone and metal flow is bigger still.** India alone exports $12B of cut-and-polished diamonds, and the US imports a similar order of magnitude. - **[E] Lab-grown price deflation** (from $4,622 to $2,057 per carat in six years, and about $845 wholesale for 1 carat) shrinks the value of each transaction. That reduces take-rate income per stone but increases the number of stones. Per-stone software (inventory, certificates, listings) becomes relatively more valuable than revenue based on value.

## 5. AI-native vertical software: where an AI-first entrant took share from incumbents, and what made it work

### Takeaway
The clearest cases (2023–2026) are in legal and healthcare documentation:
- **Harvey:** ARR from $50M to about $190M during 2025.
- **EvenUp:** sells finished work, not seats.
- **Abridge:** valued at $5.3B in June 2025.

What worked:
- selling against labour budgets, not IT budgets;
- charging per outcome or per unit of work;
- owning permissioned proprietary data.

Incumbents respond by bundling AI and buying data. Clio, for example, bought vLex for $1B.

### Cited Findings
- **Harvey:** ARR about **$190M at the end of 2025, from $50M at the end of 2024**. A less reliable blog claims $300M by mid-2026 [S] ([Vertical GTM Guild](https://verticalgtmguild.substack.com/p/the-displacement-data-is-in); [ai2roi](https://ai2roi.substack.com/p/ai-to-roi-big-story-the-power-of)).
- **EvenUp** (personal injury law): acts as an outsourced provider that sells work, priced against paralegal staff costs. Critics question how defensible the wedge is [S] ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/7dc4087e-578a-42c8-bc2a-9367676eb0c3/)).
- **Abridge** (clinical notes): **$300M Series E at a $5.3B valuation** in June 2025, led by a16z and Khosla. ARR not confirmed [S] (search summary of [BVP](https://bvp.com/atlas/building-vertical-ai-an-early-stage-playbook-for-founders) and related).
- **What made it work, according to the essays:**
  - "Vertical AI isn't competing for IT budgets; it's competing for labor budgets."
  - The shift is from seats to completed work.
  - "The winner is the one with the deepest permissioned dataset."
  - Incumbents bundle AI as a feature; the AI-native company must win through focus.

  [S] ([BVP Building Vertical AI, January 2026](https://www.bvp.com/assets/uploads/2026/01/BUILDING-VERTICAL-AI_PDF_BESSEMER_VENTURE_PARTNERS_BOOK_JANUARY_2026.pdf); [Primary](https://www.primary.vc/articles/vertical-ai-isnt-vertical-software-with-a-chatbot); [a16z, The incumbents are coming](https://a16z.com/the-incumbents-are-coming/))
- a16z: AI could raise revenue per customer by 2–10x (forecast) [S] ([a16z](https://a16z.com/vertical-saas-now-with-ai-inside/)).
- Tidemark: the next stage after the system of record is the "system of action", where agents act on the data [S] ([Tidemark](https://www.tidemarkcap.com/vskp-chapter/the-race-to-system-of-action)).
- **Incumbent response:** Clio bought vLex for $1B and raised $500M at a $5B valuation. Dealroom frames this as "legal AI becomes a platform fight" [S] ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/128915-clio-crosses-500m-arr-as-legal-ai-becomes-a-platform-fight/)).
- **Jewelry-specific AI:** BLNG, Gemist, Arcade and Skyveil are all in design. None is yet in trade operations [S] (see question 3).
- **Sarine as a warning:** an incumbent built on technology for natural diamonds lost 25% of revenue when lab-grown changed the trade [S] (see question 3).

### Inferences
- **[E] Labour budgets in this trade** that an AI-native entrant could sell against:
  - **CAD designers:** the 2021 seat price was about $3–6k, but the labour cost is a full salary. Gemist claims 14,000 pieces designed weekly.
  - **Stock listing and data-entry clerks:** uploading stones to Rapaport, VDB and Nivoda, and matching certificates.
  - **Memo and consignment tracking.**
  - **Indian GST and HUID billing back offices.**
  - **Sales staff matching requests** ("find me a 1.5ct F VS1"), which is a natural agent task over marketplace feeds.
- **[E] The incumbents are weak on AI.** They are:
  - founder-led or newly PE-owned (The Edge);
  - shrinking (Sarine);
  - low-cost Indian billing tools;
  - perpetual-licence CAD.

  None has a strong data moat except Rapaport's price list and GIA's grading data, and GIA is now tied to Tracr.
- **[E] The permissioned dataset** in this trade is each firm's stock, memo and certificate records, together with the trading feeds. An AI-native system of record that collects those across manufacturers, dealers and retailers would have the "deepest permissioned dataset" that investors cite.

### Gaps
- No jewelry-specific case of an AI-native entrant taking share from an incumbent was found.
- Harvey, Abridge and EvenUp figures are from secondary sources only.
- Other verticals (for example AI-native accounting, dental, and home-services voice agents against ServiceTitan) were not searched (budget).
