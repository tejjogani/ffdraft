## 1. Diamond manufacturing software (natural): ERPs, process tracking, rough planning, yield and costing

### Takeaway
Manufacturing splits into two layers that barely talk to each other. (1) **Rough scanning and planning** is a hardware-plus-software duopoly. Sarine (Israel; Galaxy inclusion scanners plus the Advisor planner, with a self-reported 25,000+ Advisor installations) and Lexus/OctoNus (Surat/Russia; Helium scanners, from EUR 30,000) hold it, and both use proprietary file formats (.adv, .cap). (2) **Factory ERP** (packet/kapan issue and return across departments, weight loss, piece-rate labour, lab issue and receive) is a long tail of small Surat software houses selling custom or semi-custom Windows/SQL-style systems through IndiaMART. A few of the largest groups run SAP. None publishes customer counts, prices or architecture.

### Inferences
- [E] The manufacturing ERP market in Surat is fragmented and local. Dozens of small vendors sell through IndiaMART and word of mouth, often customised per factory. Typical stacks are Windows desktop or client-server on SQL Server/Oracle (the vendors' own skills listings point that way). No vendor has a national brand or a published customer count. A "leading vendor by number of firms" cannot be named from public sources.
- [E] The planner (Sarine/OctoNus) is the system of record for "what this rough can yield". The ERP is the system of record for "where this packet is and what it weighs". The handover between them is file export or manual re-keying of planned weight and value per piece. A new system that reads planner outputs (.adv/.cap or their export CSVs) and carries planned yield against actual per process would close a visible gap.
- [E] From background knowledge, not verified this session: the core factory concepts are kapan/lot, packet, department issue and receive (galaxy/planning, laser sawing, bruting, polishing/ghat, 4P, assortment), expected vs actual weight per stage, worker-wise piece rates by size and process, and lab issue and receive. Vendor feature lists above match this vocabulary.
- [E] Rough-to-polished yield and costing (rough cost plus labour plus overhead, allocated to polished pieces by planned value) appears to be done in the ERP or in Excel. No vendor page found shows a standard costing model.

## 2. Lab-grown specifics: growing runs, as-grown to polished, stone counts, price collapse

### Takeaway
Lab-grown growers are a newer, larger-volume segment. India has roughly 8,000–10,000 CVD reactors (industry estimate). Kira alone has 4,000 reactors and makes 250,000+ polished carats a month. Growers have started buying reactor-level MES/ERP from small vendors (Upstair, Silverstar Grow, iFactory). Price collapse is the defining data problem: wholesale lab-grown prices fell about 26% year on year in 2025 and 42% for 1–3 ct over a year to Q2 2025. Stock bought months earlier is worth far less, and no product found handles revaluation explicitly.

### Inferences
- [E] Lab-grown-specific systems exist but are young, small and reactor-centric (MES). They stop at the as-grown crystal. After growing, the stone enters the same Surat cutting ERPs as natural rough. As-grown to polished tracking (crystal, run, reactor, seed plate, then polished stones and certificate) is a linking problem no vendor visibly solves end to end.
- [E] Stone counts: 250,000 polished carats a month at one grower, many under 1 ct, means hundreds of thousands to millions of stones a month at the largest growers. Desktop ERPs built around a few thousand packets will strain. Bulk operations, parcel-level handling and fast feed generation matter more than per-stone screens.
- [E] Valuation: with 26–42% annual price falls, inventory at cost overstates value. A system of record needs mark-to-market (re-price stock against a current price list or the firm's own sell prices), lower-of-cost-or-market reporting, and ageing by grow date. No vendor found advertises this.
- [E] Rapaport's exclusion of synthetics means lab-grown cannot be priced as "discount from Rap" on Rapaport's own platform, nor syndicated to it. Lab-grown pricing is usually per-carat or per-stone, sometimes against IDEX or private lists, and syndication goes to Nivoda, IDEX, VDB and retailer feeds. A system has to support more than one pricing basis per stone type.

## 3. Diamond trading software: inventory, memo, holds, pricing, certificates, media, syndication

### Takeaway
Trading software is a mature, crowded, mostly older category. Antwerp's DiamTrade (HotSoft, since 1998; desktop about USD 5,000/yr, cloud about USD 750/yr) and US tools such as DiamondTrack Online, Accadia DiamondXecutive and White Diamond Pro all do stock, memo, invoicing and upload to RapNet/IDEX. Marketplaces have moved to APIs: Rapaport Trade upload API/FTP, IDEX feeds, the Nivoda GraphQL API plus supplier API/FTP, and GIA Report Results GraphQL. Certificate APIs exist only for GIA publicly. Media comes from V360 and Sarine as hosted links.

## 4. Data standards and how messy real-world data is

### Takeaway
There is no industry-body standard for loose-diamond inventory data that the search could find. The de facto standards are the marketplaces' own field lists. The RapNet/Rapaport Trade "accepted headers and values" list is the most widely copied, and it maps many header aliases and value codes to one field, which shows how varied incoming files are. IDEX uses fixed enumerations. Unsupported fields are silently dropped, and rows missing mandatory fields are rejected.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Rapaport Trade maps many header aliases to one field. Stock number accepts "ReferenceNum, ReferenceNumber, Stock, Stock Num, Stock_no, StockNo, StockNum, StockNumber, VenderStockNumber". Length, width and depth each have several alternate names. — [Rapaport Trade fields and values](https://help.rapaport.com/en/articles/5469769-rapaport-trade-formerly-rapnet-diamond-fields-values); [RapNet TechNet accepted headers and values](https://technet.rapaport.com/Info/LotUpload/FieldsAndValues.aspx)
- [S] Shape codes vary. Trillion-type shapes appear as "TRILL, TRIB, TRL, MTRI, TRMB, TRSC", with "Other = X". — [Rapaport Trade fields and values](https://help.rapaport.com/en/articles/5469769-rapaport-trade-formerly-rapnet-diamond-fields-values)
- [S] Diamonds missing mandatory fields or values "do not upload". Fields or values outside the accepted list "are not included in the diamond item details" (silently dropped). Numeric fields must not contain commas, "$" or "%". — [RapNet: upload intro](https://help.rapnet.com/en/articles/2982445-introduction-to-uploading-diamonds-for-sale-on-rapnet); [Rapaport Trade fields and values](https://help.rapaport.com/en/articles/5469769-rapaport-trade-formerly-rapnet-diamond-fields-values)
- [S] IDEX uses full shape names (Round, Marquise, Pear, Oval, Heart, Emerald…). Its colour field takes letter grades plus trade categories (Collection, White, TLC, LC, Cape, TTLB, TLB, LB). — [IDEX Direct Inventory](https://api.idexonline.com/DiamondTradingNetwork/IdexDirectInventory)
- [S] White Diamond Pro flags items that do not meet each platform's upload standard. Vendors build per-platform validation. — [White Diamond Pro](http://whitediamondpro.com/feature.php)
- [S] RapNet de-listed EGL-graded diamonds (pre-2024). Lab policy is itself a data rule (allowed lab values per platform). — [National Jeweler: RapNet to delist EGL](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/diamonds-gems/grading/1063-rapnet-to-delist-eglgraded-diamonds)

### Inferences
- [E] A new system needs a canonical stone model (shape, carat, colour including fancy colour intensity and overtone, clarity, cut, polish, symmetry, fluorescence intensity and colour, measurements, depth, table, crown/pavilion, girdle, culet, lab, report number, natural vs lab-grown with growth method, location, price per carat, discount, total, media URLs). It also needs inbound alias maps (the RapNet list is a ready dictionary of real-world variants) and outbound per-channel serialisers with pre-flight validation. That is the "normaliser" every marketplace has rebuilt privately.
- [E] Supplier files mix price per carat and Rap discount, use different shape and colour codes, and carry free-text comments, key-to-symbols and BGM/milky/shade fields with no standard values. The aliases above suggest the mess is large. No quantified error rate was found.

### Gaps
- No industry-body standard found (WFDB, IDMA, GJEPC, AWDC, RJC or GIA). The search did not find any; a targeted search on GJEPC/AWDC data standards or GS1 might.
- No measured data-quality statistics (share of rows rejected or corrected).
