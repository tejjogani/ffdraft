## 1. Platform image specifications

### Takeaway
One square master render of about 4000 px, exported as sRGB JPEG/PNG derivatives of 2000 to 2048 px on a pure white (255,255,255) ground with the piece filling about 85% of the frame, satisfies Shopify, Amazon, Google, Meta and Kohl's-style retail specs at once. The hard blockers for render-only catalogues are not pixel specs: Etsy now requires at least one unedited photograph of the actual item, Google bans mockups/illustrations "that don't depict the physical product" and requires IPTC tagging of generative-AI images, and Amazon moderators say "no illustrations" without saying whether a photoreal render counts.

### Cited Findings

- [V] Zalando jewellery guide: portrait 1:1.44, 1801 x 2600 px best practice (762 x 1100 minimum), JPEG up to 20 MB in sRGB, white background with light shadows for the primary packshot, at least three distinct images. — [Zalando Partner University: Jewellery image guide](https://partner.zalando.com/university/article/jewellery-image-guide)

### Inferences
- [E] Shopify's CDN serves WebP or AVIF automatically when the browser's `Accept` header allows it, and the `image_url` filter takes `width`/`height`/`format` parameters; the help page only confirms "best supported format" [V], so the AVIF detail is from working knowledge.
- [E] Shopify zoom is a theme feature, not a platform one; most themes show the original up to about 2048 px in a lightbox, so a 2048 px upload gives roughly 2x zoom on a 1000 px gallery slot. Uploading 4000+ px gains zoom depth only if the theme requests larger sizes.
- [E] Amazon's canonical page (G1881) is commonly summarised as: 1,600 px or larger on the longest side for best zoom, zoom starts at 1,000 px, main image must not be cropped by the frame edge with an exception for jewelry such as necklaces, no mannequins. I could not open that page, so none of this is verified today.
- [E] Amazon does not publicly state a rule naming photoreal renders. The verified language is "actual product", "no illustrations", "professional photography" and "accurately represent" [V]. A faithful, photoreal CAD render of the exact SKU is widely used in practice, but it is a risk a seller carries; a stylised or visibly synthetic render is the kind of image the "no illustrations" line targets.
- [E] Prior to the 500 px rule, Google's minimum was 100 x 100 px (250 x 250 for apparel); the pages read today no longer show those figures.
- A render-only Etsy listing does not meet Etsy's policy as written: the unedited-photo rule asks for a photograph of the actual (or, for made-to-order, a previously made) item [V]. Renders could sit in the remaining 19 slots if they represent the piece accurately.
- For Google, the tagging duty attaches to generative AI [V]. A path-traced CAD render is not generative AI, so `TrainedAlgorithmicMedia` would be the wrong tag; whether Google treats a faithful render as a permitted depiction of "the physical product" or as a "mockup" is not stated [V that it is unstated].
- Safe common-denominator export for the render workflow [E]: square 1:1; master 4000 x 4000 px; publish 2048 px (Shopify), 2000+ px (Etsy, Kohl's-style), 1600 to 2000 px (Amazon), 1500+ px (Google), 1024+ px (Meta); a 2:3 crop at 1000 x 1500 for Pinterest; sRGB with embedded profile; JPEG for marketplaces.

## 2. Shot list and visual conventions for fine-jewelry pages

### Takeaway
The shared convention is a white or near-white packshot hero plus alternates: an angle view, a detail macro, a scale or on-body image, packaging, and a short video, with every colour variant getting its own main image. Baymard's testing says scale is the image type shoppers most often miss, and worn products need a model image.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Kohl's cadence by type. Rings: main straight/front, angle, detail, open box, closed box, scale/on-model, educational, video. Earrings: main front, detail, boxes, scale/on-model, educational, video; a pair is shown with the left earring straight on and the right turned to show detail. Necklaces: main front (pendant chokers also a laydown), detail, boxes, scale/on-model, educational, video. Bracelets: main front, detail, packaging, scale/on-model, educational. — [Kohl's Vendor Style Guide: Jewelry](https://link.kohls.com/content/dam/klink/pdfs/Vendor-Style-Guide-Jewelry.pdf)
- [V] Kohl's framing rules: square crop tight to the product, at least 85% fill, a 50 to 200 px cushion on a 2000 px image, pure white with no grounding shadow or reflection for jewelry (shadow kept only for boxes and trays). — [Kohl's Vendor Style Guide: Jewelry](https://link.kohls.com/content/dam/klink/pdfs/Vendor-Style-Guide-Jewelry.pdf)
- [V] Zalando's example sets. Rings: primary, model, top, side. Necklaces: primary, model, back, detail. Bracelets: primary, model, two details. Earrings: primary, front, two details. White background with light shadows "to show detail and symmetry"; coloured backgrounds only on additional views; the finish (matt or shiny) must be visible; show clasps and the inside; avoid overexposure; duplicated images are rejected. — [Zalando Partner University: Jewellery image guide](https://partner.zalando.com/university/article/jewellery-image-guide)
- [V] Amazon asks for additional images that show the product in use, from other angles and highlighting features, and recommends six images plus one video. — [Seller Forums: Quick Tip, Product Images](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/ab884127-f9b4-4053-8096-4991e1d60d1f); [Seller Forums: Listings Lounge](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/4b3c4c39-6f8c-4312-aa0e-99982eb8f5e1)
- [V] Google recommends a white or light neutral main image, 75 to 90% fill, several additional angles and at least one lifestyle image. — [Google Merchant Center Help: Image best practices](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/18353357?hl=en)
- [V] Baymard, scale: 42% of test users tried to judge size from product images; 28% of benchmarked sites had no in-scale image in the 2017 article, and 37% in the product-page article updated 18 March 2026; cut-out-only galleries led users to misjudge size and sometimes discard suitable products; users often skipped videos, so video supplements rather than replaces an in-scale still; computer-generated in-scale graphics built from the product's dimensions are an accepted fallback, ideally alongside real photography. — [Baymard: Provide at least one "In Scale" image](https://baymard.com/blog/in-scale-product-images); [Baymard: Product page UX best practices 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- [V] Baymard, human model: 23% of sites lack model images; worn products, with jewelry and watches named, need a model to show how they look and fit. — [Baymard: Product page UX best practices 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- [V] Baymard, zoom: exploring images was the first action on a product page for 56% of users; 25% of sites had too little resolution or zoom (14% low resolution, 11% insufficient zoom); users zoom to judge material and build quality; on mobile, load a higher-resolution file once zooming starts. — [Baymard: Ensure sufficient image resolution and zoom (2020)](https://baymard.com/blog/ensure-sufficient-image-resolution-and-zoom)
- [V] Baymard, mobile gestures (2016, old benchmark): 40% of top mobile sites supported neither pinch nor double-tap on product images; support both and hint that zoom exists. — [Baymard: Mobile image gestures](https://baymard.com/blog/mobile-image-gestures)
- [V] Baymard's older taxonomy of useful image types includes textural close-ups, size-and-proportion images (product beside a familiar object or a hand), lifestyle and customer images. — [Baymard: 7 types of product images (2012)](https://baymard.com/blog/ux-product-image-categories)

### Inferences
All points below are [E]: working knowledge of the public product pages of Tiffany, Cartier, Blue Nile, Brilliant Earth, James Allen, Mejuri and Catbird. I did not open those product pages today (galleries are script-rendered and the search budget went to primary rule pages), so treat this as a description of conventions to be spot-checked.
- Hero angle by type: rings at a three-quarter view from slightly above with the head toward the camera (Blue Nile, Brilliant Earth, James Allen), sometimes a straight-on "through finger" or top-down view as the second image; stud and drop earrings as a matched pair, one frontal and one turned; pendants hanging on their chain with the chain cropped or looped at the top; bracelets either closed in an oval loop seen from three-quarter or laid straight; bangles upright at three-quarter.
- Standard alternates for rings: top (face-up), side profile showing gallery and basket height, through-finger profile, detail macro of setting/pavé/hallmark, on-hand shot, and often a stacking or pairing shot.
- Scale: on-hand, on-ear and on-neck images dominate; rulers and coins are rare at the luxury end and common on marketplaces; Blue Nile, Brilliant Earth and James Allen let the shopper pick carat size or skin tone on a hand view; dimension diagrams (width, height, drop length, chain length on a bust outline) are common for pendants and earrings.
- Metal variants: each metal colour is a separate, pixel-aligned image set (same camera, lighting and crop) so swapping the swatch does not move the piece; engagement-ring sites also swap centre-stone shape.
- Background: mass and diamond-specialist sites use pure white or a barely off-white; luxury houses (Tiffany, Cartier) use white or very light grey with a soft contact shadow; fashion-fine brands (Mejuri, Catbird) mix white or warm off-white packshots with skin-tone lifestyle images. Marketplace feeds need the pure white version regardless.
- Shadow: a soft contact shadow directly under the piece is the usual choice; mirror reflections under the piece look dated and are barred by some retail guides [V Kohl's]; no shadow at all is required for Kohl's-type feeds and safest for Amazon.
- Consistency: constant fill, centring, camera height and light direction across the catalogue so grid pages read as one system.
- Count: about 5 to 8 stills per product, plus one spin or video, is typical; this sits inside Amazon's 6+1 advice [V] and Google's 3 to 5+ additional images [V].
- 360: James Allen, Blue Nile and Brilliant Earth are known for interactive 360 views of rings and loose diamonds (image-sequence spins or video scrubbing); luxury houses more often use a short autoplay loop.
- Workflow implication [E]: "stills in several settings" should map to (1) pure-white marketplace packshot, (2) own-site packshot on brand background with contact shadow, (3) detail macro, (4) profile/top technical views, (5) scale image (on-body or dimensioned diagram), (6) per-metal variants from a locked camera.

### Gaps
- No quantitative research was found on the optimum number of product images, or on conversion effects of 360 spins; Baymard's open articles read today do not cover 360 views or video beyond noting that users often skip video. Nielsen Norman Group material was not opened.
- Retailer product pages were not opened, so no convention above is [V] for a named retailer.

## 3. Truthfulness, scale and disclosure when the image is a render

### Takeaway
US and Indian law do not regulate "renders" as such: the test is whether the picture, taken as a whole, misleads about a material attribute (size, colour, quality, stone type). The one image-specific US rule is that a diamond or gemstone shown larger than life needs its true size disclosed. Faithful CAD renders and generative-AI images are treated differently mainly by platforms (Google's IPTC tag, Etsy's unedited-photo rule) and by India's draft ASCI AI-labelling guidance, not by the FTC Jewelry Guides.

### Cited Findings

**United States: FTC Jewelry Guides, 16 CFR Part 23** (eCFR text shown as current to 20 July 2026; Part 23 source 83 FR 40667, 16 Aug 2018)

### Inferences
- Is a faithful CAD render treated differently from an AI-generated image? Under the FTC Guides and the Indian statute, no category exists for either; both are "depictions" judged by net impression [V for the standard]. The difference is practical [E]: a CAD render is a deterministic picture of the manufacturing geometry and can be verified against the spec sheet, while a generative image can invent prongs, stone counts or proportions. Platform rules do distinguish: Google's tagging duty attaches to generative AI [V], Etsy's AI rules sit beside an unedited-photo requirement that a render also fails [V], ASCI's draft labels AI visuals that show products that do not yet exist [V].
- Made-to-order risk [E]: a render of a piece that has never been manufactured is, in ASCI's draft language, close to a demonstration of a product that does not yet exist, though that draft is about AI, not CGI. The conservative reading is to say so plainly near the gallery.
- Where renders most easily mislead [E]: (a) stone size, since macro framing enlarges every stone and § 23.1 Note 3 then calls for true size (mm and carat weight) near the image [V]; (b) diamond appearance, since a path tracer shows an idealised colourless, flawless, ideally cut stone while the delivered stone has a stated colour/clarity grade; (c) metal colour, since 14k versus 18k yellow and rhodium-plated versus unplated white gold differ in reality; (d) polish and finish, since renders have no tool marks or porosity; (e) on-body scale, when a CG hand or ear is not at true proportion; (f) lab-grown origin, which must be in the title/description next to the stone name, not only in a footer [V for proximity].
- Suggested disclosure practice [E], for a lawyer to confirm: a short line directly under the gallery such as "Images are computer renderings of this design, enlarged to show detail; see dimensions below. Natural variation in stones and metal colour applies", actual dimensions (mm) and carat weight in the first screen of product details, a dimensioned diagram or true-scale on-body image in the gallery, and a fuller statement in the site's terms/disclosures page. A footer-only or terms-only notice is weak against the "close to the claim" factor in § 23.1 Note 2 and ASCI's proximity rule [V for those factors]. In India the line must be in the same language and font as the claim it qualifies [V].
- A disclaimer cannot rescue a render that departs from the product (extra stones, larger centre stone, different colour); FTC guidance, the CCPA guidelines and ASCI all say a disclosure cannot correct a misleading claim [V].
- Verification-report content that follows from the above [E]: rendered stone diameters and counts matched to the spec sheet; render camera scale recorded and any magnification factor stated; metal preset matched to the alloy sold; a statement that stones are rendered as idealised; on-body scale checked against real anatomical dimensions; lab-grown wording present beside the stone name.
- [E] New York amended its General Business Law (signed December 2025, in force around June 2026) to require conspicuous disclosure when an advertisement uses a "synthetic performer" created with generative AI or a software algorithm; together with California SB 1050 [V via Etsy], this matters only if on-model images use an AI or CG human. A rendered hand or ear may or may not be a "performer". LAWYER.
- [E] EU and UK, briefly: the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC and the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (which replaced the 2008 Regulations in April 2025) prohibit misleading actions and omissions about a product's main characteristics, including by overall presentation; the UK CAP Code rule 3.1 says ads must not materially mislead. The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency duties for synthetic content apply from August 2026 and target deepfakes and AI-generated media rather than conventional CGI. None opened today.
- [E] BIS hallmarking in India governs the marks on the article (BIS logo, purity, six-character HUID) and sale of hallmarked gold; I know of no rule on product imagery, but a render must not show a hallmark or purity stamp the delivered piece will not carry, and purity/karat claims in listings must match the hallmark.

## 4. Colour accuracy and consistency

### Takeaway
Deliver everything as 8-bit sRGB with the profile embedded, and composite the "white" background to exactly 255,255,255 after tone mapping, because a tone-mapped render does not reach pure white on its own. Catalogue-wide metal colour is a process control (locked materials, lighting and view transform), not something any platform enforces.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Platforms that state a colour space: Etsy says convert to sRGB before upload (CMYK is for print); Pinterest says upload sRGB and converts everything to 8-bit RGB JPEG; Zalando requires sRGB; Kohl's accepts sRGB or Adobe RGB; Amazon's clothing style guide prefers RGB and warns CMYK can show tonal shifts. — [Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015663347-Requirements-and-Best-Practices-for-Images-in-Your-Etsy-Shop); [Pinterest Help](https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-product-specs); [Zalando](https://partner.zalando.com/university/article/jewellery-image-guide); [Kohl's](https://link.kohls.com/content/dam/klink/pdfs/Vendor-Style-Guide-Jewelry.pdf); [Amazon Clothing Style Guide](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/65/SG3P/LISTING_GUIDES/Amazon_Fashion_Clothing_Image_Style_Guide.pdf)
- [V] White backgrounds: Amazon states RGB 255,255,255 for the main image; Kohl's states pure white RGB 255 with no grounding shadow or reflection; Google asks for solid white or light neutral without a numeric value. — [sell.amazon.com](https://sell.amazon.com/blog/product-photos); [Kohl's](https://link.kohls.com/content/dam/klink/pdfs/Vendor-Style-Guide-Jewelry.pdf); [Google: Image best practices](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/18353357?hl=en)
- [V] Tone mapping and white: in `<model-viewer>`'s default setup a matte white sphere renders between about 177 and 237 per channel and never reaches 255; rendered pixel colour is not expected to equal the material's base colour because lighting, exposure and tone mapping all act on it. ACES skews hue and desaturates; AgX holds hue better but loses contrast and saturation; the Khronos PBR Neutral tone mapper was designed for e-commerce colour fidelity and is recommended with greyscale lighting. — [modelviewer.dev: Achieving color-accurate presentation](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/color)
- [V] Google requires variant images to reflect the exact colour, finish and material of that variant; Zalando requires the shown colour to match the article and the finish to be visible. — [Google: image_link](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324350?hl=en); [Zalando](https://partner.zalando.com/university/article/jewellery-image-guide)
- [V] Etsy bars edits that change a product's actual colour or texture, while allowing colour-balance edits that bring the image closer to real life. — [Etsy: Listing Image Requirements](https://www.etsy.com/legal/policy/listing-image-requirements/253962679005)

### Inferences
All [E] unless marked.
- Most marketplaces and image CDNs re-encode uploads and may strip metadata including the ICC profile; a browser treats an untagged image as sRGB. So converting to sRGB and embedding the small sRGB profile gives the same result whether or not the profile survives. Wide-gamut (Display P3, Adobe RGB) uploads risk a desaturated or shifted yellow/rose gold after stripping.
- Stripping metadata also bears on Google's rule that IPTC `DigitalSourceType` must be preserved for generative-AI images [V]; if any AI-assisted image is used, check that the storefront's CDN does not drop IPTC/XMP from the file Google fetches.
- Hitting exactly 255,255,255: render the piece with a transparent or holdout background and a shadow catcher, apply the view transform to the piece only, then composite over a flat #FFFFFF plate in display space. Filmic or AgX applied to a white backdrop yields light grey, in line with the model-viewer note [V]. Verify numerically: all four borders and corner blocks equal 255 in all channels; for JPEG check after export, since chroma subsampling and ringing can leave 253 to 254 pixels next to edges (use quality 90+ and 4:4:4, or flatten near-white to white with a tight threshold that does not eat highlights on polished metal).
- A contact shadow conflicts with a strict "pure white, no grounding shadow" spec [V Kohl's]; keep two outputs, marketplace (no shadow, pure 255) and own-site (soft shadow or off-white).
- Metal consistency: fixed material presets per alloy (14k/18k yellow, rose, white rhodium-plated, platinum), one HDRI and light rig, one exposure and one view transform across the catalogue; record target sRGB patch values for a reference sphere or ring per alloy and report the difference (for example CIEDE2000) in the verification report. Gold hue in a render comes mostly from the environment reflection, so changing the HDRI changes "the gold".
- Banding on soft grey gradients and shadows is the common 8-bit artefact; render at 16-bit, dither when converting to 8-bit, and avoid very low AVIF/WebP quality on gradient backgrounds.

### Gaps
- No platform page read today documents how it strips or converts ICC profiles (Shopify, Amazon, Google, Meta all silent). The stripping behaviour above is unverified.
- No public standard was found for target sRGB values of gold alloys in product imagery.

## 5. Delivery and performance on the page

### Takeaway
Serve the gallery's first still as a normal, eagerly loaded, high-priority `<img>` with `srcset`, lazy-load everything else, and treat spin, video and 3D as on-demand extras behind a poster. For photoreal jewelry a pre-rendered image-sequence spin or a muted looping MP4 beats real-time 3D, because glTF viewers cannot reproduce gemstone internal reflection.

### Cited Findings

**Images**
- [V] Images are roughly half of median page weight, and on more than 70% of pages the largest element in the first viewport is an image, so LCP is usually an image problem; set `width` and `height` to prevent layout shift; do not lazy-load above-the-fold images; mark the LCP image `fetchpriority="high"`. — [web.dev: Learn Images, key performance issues](https://web.dev/learn/images/performance-issues)
- [V] `fetchpriority="high"` on the LCP image cut LCP from 2.6 s to 1.9 s in Google's Flights test; in a carousel only the first slide should be high and the rest low; supported in Chrome 102, Edge 102, Firefox 132, Safari 17.2. — [web.dev: Fetch Priority API](https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority)
- [V] `loading="lazy"` belongs only on images outside the first viewport; Chrome begins fetching lazy images 1250 px before the viewport on fast connections and 2500 px on slow ones; without `width`/`height` a gallery can collapse and load every image at once; images hidden with `display:none` are not fetched; supported from Chrome 77, Firefox 75, Safari 15.4. — [web.dev: Browser-level image lazy loading](https://web.dev/articles/browser-level-image-lazy-loading)
- [V] Responsive markup: `srcset` with width descriptors plus `sizes`, or density descriptors (1x, 2x, 3x), never both in one attribute; keep a valid `src`; `<picture>` with AVIF then WebP sources and a JPEG fallback; keep the LCP image as a plain `<img>` in the HTML rather than injected by script or set as a CSS background. An example in the MDN post took a 1 MB image to 46 kB as AVIF at quality 80. — [web.dev: Responsive images](https://web.dev/learn/design/responsive-images); [MDN blog: Fix image LCP (Jan 2025)](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/blog/fix-image-lcp/)
- [V] Image CDNs resize, convert and compress by URL parameters and typically save 40 to 80% of image bytes; "auto" format picks AVIF, WebP or JPEG per browser; prefer a CDN proxied through the site's own origin to avoid an extra connection for the LCP image. — [web.dev: Use image CDNs](https://web.dev/articles/image-cdns)
- [V] Shopify does the CDN part itself: per-image size variants and browser-appropriate format; themes request sizes through `image_url` and can ask for a 2x thumbnail with `scale: 2`. — [Shopify Help: Product media types](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/product-media-types); [shopify.dev: Support product media](https://shopify.dev/docs/storefronts/themes/product-merchandising/media/support-media)

**360 spins**

- [V] Shopify's `model_viewer_tag` outputs Google's `<model-viewer>`; AR goes through AR Quick Look (USDZ) on iOS and Scene Viewer on Android. - [V] Why gems look poor: Khronos's ratified `KHR_materials_dispersion` extension (diamond listed at Abbe number 55, dispersion 0.36) notes that gemstones have many internal reflections and that dispersion alone does not capture their look in a real-time rasteriser. A Shopify merchant reported diamonds in GLB/USDZ looking transparent and depthless, and a reply said real-time formats do not handle diamonds well. A three.js developer published real-time diamond shaders in July 2026 using internal-reflection and RGB-dispersion tricks. — [Threekit: Gem material](https://community.threekit.com/platform-documentation/project-data/assets/materials/gem-material); [WANNA: Gems production](https://wanna.helpjuice.com/jewelry/gems-production); [Shopify Community thread](https://community.shopify.com/t/problem-with-adding-real-life-diamond-jewelry-3d-model/324145); [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/i-made-two-real-time-diamond-shaders/92948)

### Inferences
All [E] unless marked.
- Still-image encoding for jewelry: JPEG quality 82 to 90 with 4:4:4 chroma (4:2:0 softens coloured gem edges and fine prongs against white); WebP quality 80 to 90 (lossy WebP is always 4:2:0, so judge coloured-stone edges at 200%); AVIF quality about 60 to 75 with 4:4:4, and 10-bit where the pipeline allows, which is the best protection against banding in soft shadows and grey gradients. Keep the JPEG as the universal fallback. Expect a 2048 px white-ground packshot at about 150 to 350 kB JPEG, 80 to 200 kB WebP, 50 to 150 kB AVIF.
- `srcset` widths for a square gallery slot: 400, 600, 800, 1200, 1600, 2048 px with a `sizes` value that reflects the real slot (about 100vw on phones, about 50vw on desktop). A 2x cap is enough: 3x phones show little visible gain over 2x for the byte cost. Load a 2048+ px file only when zoom opens, as Baymard recommends for mobile [V].
- Only the first gallery image gets eager loading and high priority; thumbnails and later slides are lazy; spin frames, video and 3D scripts load on interaction or when scrolled into view, with a still poster occupying the slot so LCP is the poster image, not the video.
- Spin frame counts: 24 frames (15 degrees) looks steppy on faceted stones; 36 (10 degrees) is the usual baseline; 72 (5 degrees) is smooth and suits scintillation; more than about 40 full-size frames strains phone memory [V js-cloudimage README]. Budget: 36 frames at 1000 px and about 40 to 60 kB each (WebP/AVIF) is about 1.5 to 2 MB; 72 frames doubles that. Sprite sheets cut requests but hit decoded-size limits on iOS (a 6 x 6 sheet of 1000 px frames is 6000 x 6000 px), so individual frames over HTTP/2 or 3 are safer.
- Spin versus loop: an image-sequence spin gives drag control and sharp zoomable frames but costs megabytes and script; a 5 to 10 second turntable video is far smaller for the same motion, autoplays muted on iOS and Android [V], but cannot be dragged without scrub-by-seek tricks and shows compression shimmer on pavé. A sensible default is a looped video in the gallery with an optional interactive spin behind a "360" button.
- Turntable video targets: 1080 x 1080 (or 720 x 720 for inline mobile), 24 to 30 fps, 6 to 8 s, seamless 360 degree loop, no audio track, `+faststart`. H.264 High profile at CRF 20 to 23 gives roughly 1 to 3 MB (about 1.5 to 3 Mbit/s) on a white ground; HEVC or AV1 reach similar quality at about half that. H.264 MP4 plays everywhere and should be the fallback; HEVC (`hvc1` tag) helps Safari and hardware-decode Chrome; AV1 plays in Chrome, Firefox and Edge and in Safari only on devices with hardware AV1 decode; VP9 WebM is the non-Apple alternative. Sparkle is high-frequency detail, so jewelry needs more bitrate than a matte product; flat white backgrounds are cheap. Shopify re-encodes uploads to its own MP4/HLS renditions [V], so the encoding choice matters mainly for self-hosted files.
- Transparent video is rarely worth it for a product gallery: render the turntable on the same background colour as the page slot (pure white or the brand tint) and use ordinary H.264. Alpha video costs two or three encodes and has mobile bugs [V]. It earns its keep only when the piece must float over a changing or gradient background.
- Real-time 3D: fine for metal-only pieces and for AR "see it on my hand/desk" scale checks; for diamond pieces in stock `<model-viewer>` or AR Quick Look, stones read as glass. Options are to accept a stylised gem with a clear "3D preview" label, bake the look into textures, or use a specialised gem viewer. Under Shopify's 15 MB no-optimisation threshold [V], author GLB with Draco/Meshopt geometry and KTX2 or WebP textures and test Shopify's auto-converted USDZ separately. A 3D preview that understates sparkle is a presentation risk rather than a deception risk, but it should not be the only view.

### Gaps
- Codec support tables (caniuse, MDN video codecs) were not opened; the codec/browser statements are [E].
- No source read gives jewelry-specific encoder settings or measured file sizes; the size and bitrate figures are estimates to be checked against a real turntable.
- Licences of commercial spinners (WebRotate 360, Sirv, Magic 360, the @3dweb viewer's terms) were not opened.
- No measured data found on conversion or engagement effect of spin versus video versus 3D for jewelry.

## 6. Accessibility and SEO basics for product images

### Takeaway
Give every product image a short descriptive alt text and a crawlable, stable URL, and list the product's images (ideally several aspect ratios) in Product structured data; Google also accepts a 3D model reference in that markup.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Shopify alt text: 512 characters maximum, 125 or fewer recommended, short and descriptive; it serves screen-reader users, appears if media fails to load, and can help SEO; bulk upload by CSV is possible. — [Shopify Help: Adding alt text to media](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/add-alt-text)
- [V] web.dev: always include `alt`; use empty `alt=""` for purely decorative images. — [web.dev: Responsive images](https://web.dev/learn/design/responsive-images)
- [V] Google merchant-listing structured data: `image` is a required property of `Product` (URL or `ImageObject`, repeatable); image URLs must be crawlable and indexable and must represent the marked-up product; photos that clearly show the product are preferred (the example is on white); several high-resolution images of at least 50,000 pixels in area are recommended, in 16x9, 4x3 and 1x1; the page shows a `subjectOf` `3DModel` example pointing to a glTF file. — [Google Search Central: Merchant listing structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing)
- [V] Google Merchant feed image URLs must be reachable by Googlebot and Googlebot-image, stable (no session or timestamp parameters), and under 2,000 characters; Meta needs a new URL whenever an image changes. — [Google: image_link](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324350?hl=en); [Meta: Catalog fields](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/commerce-platform/catalog/fields)
- [V] File-naming conventions seen in retail guides: Kohl's `StyleNumber_ColorName_MAIN` / `_ALT` / `_DIMS`; Amazon `identifier.PT02.jpg` style variant codes. — [Kohl's Vendor Style Guide: Jewelry](https://link.kohls.com/content/dam/klink/pdfs/Vendor-Style-Guide-Jewelry.pdf); [Amazon Clothing Style Guide](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/G/65/SG3P/LISTING_GUIDES/Amazon_Fashion_Clothing_Image_Style_Guide.pdf)

### Inferences
All [E].
- Alt text pattern for jewelry: product type, metal and colour, stone type with origin (for example laboratory-grown diamond), view ("side profile", "on hand"); avoid keyword stuffing; do not repeat the same alt on every gallery image. Describing origin in alt text also supports the proximity principle in section 3.
- Descriptive, hyphenated, stable file names (for example `solitaire-ring-18k-yellow-gold-side.jpg`) help image search slightly; changing URLs on every re-render loses image-search history, except where Meta needs a new URL to pick up a change [V].
- For spins, video and 3D: give the control an accessible name, provide a play/pause control for any loop longer than five seconds (WCAG 2.2.2), honour `prefers-reduced-motion` by showing the poster, and give `<model-viewer>` an `alt`.
- If an image is a render and the site says so, a visible caption serves both sighted and screen-reader users better than hiding the statement in alt text.

### Gaps
- Google's supported-format list for Search images and its image SEO best-practices page were not opened; WCAG success criteria cited from memory.
- No source read states whether Google Search treats rendered product images differently from photographs in rich results.
